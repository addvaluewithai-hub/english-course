# A1 Vertical Reconciliation Plan

## Purpose

Audit the 81 source-audited A1 candidates and the current Unit 1 packages against the
cross-level spine. They are not an owner-approved syllabus and receive no preservation
bias: reuse only work that earns a place in the final curriculum.

## Candidate decision

Every A1 candidate receives exactly one primary decision:

- `keep`: outcome and control level fit A1;
- `revise`: outcome fits but scope, wording, targets, or mastery need change;
- `split`: one task cannot validly assess all parts;
- `merge`: two candidates duplicate the same independent performance;
- `defer`: independent control belongs after A1;
- `support_only`: useful language or strategy, but not separate lesson mastery;
- `replace`: the need is valid but a newly designed candidate serves it better;
- `retire`: remove the candidate because it adds no necessary independent outcome.

## Required audit columns

- candidate id, title, outcome, skills, modes, and sequence band;
- CEFR/GSE evidence refs and exact coverage claim;
- exit-profile dimensions served;
- grammar, vocabulary, chunks, and pronunciation items;
- lifecycle state and owning level for every controlled item;
- receptive versus productive requirement;
- prerequisite and recycling locations;
- fresh mastery evidence and exit-assessment contribution;
- decision, rationale, reviewer, version, and unresolved risks.

## Audit passes

### 1. Outcome and level fit

Check whether the performance belongs at A1 and whether the candidate asks for more
independence, complexity, length, or precision than the A1 exit profile requires.

### 2. Global ability coverage

Map the current 81 candidates to A1 reception, production, oral interaction, written/online
interaction, mediation, repair, and language-control expectations. Report missing and
overrepresented dimensions.

### 3. Grammar coverage

Give every A1 EGP feature a disposition. Do not require 109 separate productive
targets. Identify items that are only receptive, formulaic, supporting, optional,
overlapping, or better consolidated later.

### 4. Vocabulary and chunk coverage

Replace vague labels such as `familiar jobs` with bounded lexical selections or an
explicit authoring rule. Record chunks and collocations separately from headwords, and
identify at least one planned recycle after introduction.

### 5. Pronunciation progression

Replace isolated pronunciation mentions with an A1 intelligibility sequence covering
selected sound contrasts, word stress, sentence prominence, chunking, and repair.
Arabic-speaker risks remain hypotheses until specialist review or learner evidence.

### 6. Sequence and recycling

Confirm required prerequisites, find items that appear once and disappear, and ensure
fresh later use is scheduled before consolidation or retention is claimed.

### 7. Mastery and exit assessment

Check that a fresh task measures the actual outcome rather than vocabulary recognition,
video completion, imitation, or exercise score. Map each candidate to lesson, unit, or
level exit evidence.

## Outputs

- reviewed reconciliation dataset for all 81 current candidates, including replaced
  and retired items;
- grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, and ability coverage reports;
- proposed changes with before/after source traceability;
- final A1 unit boundaries and scope-and-sequence v1;
- owner-readable summary of what changed and why.

## Stop rule

Do not mass-author lessons during the audit. First approve the new curriculum decisions,
then author or update only the source packets and content packages the final map needs.
