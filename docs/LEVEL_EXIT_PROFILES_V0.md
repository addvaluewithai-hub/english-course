# Level Exit Profiles v0

Status: superseded proposal retained for history. The approved, claim-level,
evidence-linked contract is `docs/LEVEL_EXIT_PROFILES_V1.md` with structured data in
`data/reviewed/level-exit-profiles-v1.json`.

These profiles define exit performance, not lesson counts or a locked inventory of
grammar and vocabulary.

## Pre-A1 support band

The learner recognises highly familiar words, letters, numbers, names, and basic
expressions, and can use a very small set of rehearsed formulas with gesture, visual
support, repetition, and explicit help. Pre-A1 is used for diagnosis and remediation;
the first promised course exit remains A1.

## A1 - Supported concrete communication

- Reception: recognise familiar words, names, phrases, signs, and very simple sentences
  when speech is slow and clear and context is predictable.
- Production: give short personal details and immediate needs through isolated learnt
  phrases and simple sentences; complete very simple forms and messages.
- Interaction: ask and answer simple questions with a partner who repeats, rephrases,
  slows down, and helps formulation.
- Mediation: relay simple predictable information from signs or notices, invite a
  contribution, and indicate basic understanding or welcome.
- Language control: use a very basic concrete repertoire, a few one-clause patterns,
  and a basic lexical set with limited grammatical control.
- Pronunciation and discourse: make a small learnt repertoire intelligible to a
  collaborative listener; link items mainly with basic connectors such as `and` and
  `then`.
- Fresh exit evidence: complete a new concrete task without copying a full model;
  support may clarify the situation but may not supply the answer.

## A2 - Routine direct communication

- Reception: understand high-frequency language about immediate life and catch the
  main point of short, clear messages; find predictable information in simple everyday
  texts.
- Production: describe family, background, routines, living conditions, and immediate
  needs through a series of simple linked phrases and sentences.
- Interaction: complete routine transactions and short social exchanges through a
  direct exchange of familiar information.
- Mediation: convey the main point of short simple texts and collaborate in practical
  tasks while asking for repetition or reformulation when needed.
- Language control: use basic patterns and memorised phrases productively; systematic
  errors remain acceptable when the intended message is usually clear.
- Pronunciation and discourse: be generally understandable in familiar everyday
  situations, though repetition may be needed; connect simple sentences with frequent
  linkers such as `and`, `but`, and `because`.
- Fresh exit evidence: finish a new routine exchange and a short linked description
  with limited scaffolding and successful repair when misunderstanding occurs.

## B1 - Connected familiar independence

- Reception: understand the main points of clear standard input on familiar life,
  work, study, travel, and personal-interest topics, including relatively clear media.
- Production: produce connected descriptions and narratives, recount experiences and
  events, and give brief reasons for opinions, hopes, and plans.
- Interaction: enter unprepared conversation on familiar topics and handle most common
  travel and everyday situations without dependence on a scripted model.
- Mediation: convey information from clear well-structured familiar texts, seek simple
  compromise, and support a shared communication culture.
- Language control: use a sufficient familiar-topic repertoire with reasonable
  accuracy; hesitation, repetition, circumlocution, and mother-tongue influence remain
  visible but do not normally block meaning.
- Pronunciation and discourse: remain generally intelligible; connect shorter elements
  into a linear sequence and keep going despite noticeable planning and repair.
- Fresh exit evidence: complete an unscripted familiar problem, connected narrative,
  and reasoned response across more than one context.

## B2 - Detailed independent communication

- Reception: understand the main ideas of complex concrete and abstract texts, extended
  talk on reasonably familiar topics, most standard-language news, and relevant
  technical discussion in the learner's field.
- Production: give clear detailed descriptions, explain a viewpoint, compare options,
  and produce organised essays or reports with supporting detail.
- Interaction: participate actively and with useful fluency and spontaneity, sustain a
  position, and manage routine disagreement without imposing significant strain.
- Mediation: relay detailed information and arguments reliably, clarify complex points,
  and develop or connect other people's ideas.
- Language control: use a broad general and field-related repertoire, some complex
  sentence forms, appropriate collocations, and relatively high grammatical control;
  occasional slips remain possible.
- Pronunciation and discourse: remain intelligible throughout with accent having little
  effect; use stress, intonation, linking devices, and clear paragraphs to support the
  intended message.
- Fresh exit evidence: interpret complex input, contribute spontaneously, and produce a
  supported viewpoint for a new audience and topic within the agreed domain range.

## C1 - Flexible complex communication

- Reception: understand demanding longer texts, implicit relationships and meaning,
  complex factual and literary material, and specialised material beyond the learner's
  own field with occasional checking.
- Production: present complex subjects with integrated sub-themes and conclusions and
  produce clear, well-structured, detailed texts in an appropriate style.
- Interaction: express ideas fluently and precisely, relate contributions skilfully,
  adapt register, and resolve communication or cultural problems through clarification.
- Mediation: convey significant ideas from long complex texts, evaluate multiple
  perspectives, and guide collaboration toward coherent reasoning or a conclusion.
- Language control: choose flexibly from a broad complex repertoire with high
  grammatical accuracy, broad lexical command, idiomatic range, and effective
  circumlocution.
- Pronunciation and discourse: use the full phonological range with intelligibility
  throughout and produce smooth, cohesive, well-organised discourse.
- Fresh exit evidence: synthesize demanding input and communicate a complex position
  effectively in social, academic, and professional variants without rehearsed wording.

## C2 - Precise nuanced synthesis

- Reception: understand virtually all spoken and written language, including fast
  natural delivery after reasonable accent familiarisation and abstract, structurally
  or stylistically complex texts.
- Production: create smooth, logically structured communication that makes significant
  points memorable and differentiates fine shades of meaning for a precise audience.
- Interaction: participate effortlessly, use idiomatic and colloquial language
  appropriately, and repair or restructure so smoothly that difficulty is unobtrusive.
- Mediation: reconstruct and evaluate arguments across sources, convey nuance and
  sociocultural implications, and guide complex discussion sensitively.
- Language control: exploit a comprehensive repertoire with consistent grammatical,
  lexical, pragmatic, and stylistic control even while attention is divided.
- Pronunciation and discourse: use articulation and prosody precisely to enhance subtle
  meaning; retained accent does not affect intelligibility or effectiveness.
- Fresh exit evidence: synthesize unfamiliar complex sources and produce a precise,
  audience-appropriate response under realistic cognitive and interactional load.

## Source boundary

The profiles paraphrase the CEFR global scale, expanded self-assessment grid, language
competence scales, and phonological-control grid, cross-checked against Pearson's GSE
bands. Exact grammar, lexical, pronunciation, and ability ownership remains pending the
record-level vertical-spine audit.
