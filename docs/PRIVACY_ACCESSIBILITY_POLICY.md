# Privacy and Accessibility Policy v1

Status: locked for the learning-model and gold-lesson phases. Learner-test evidence and
later privacy, threat, and architecture reviews may revise implementation details.

## Scope

This policy sets minimum boundaries for a manual or prototype gold lesson. It does not
design production storage, accounts, consent infrastructure, or a database.

## Data minimization and learner choice

- Use fictional details by default in personal situations. Never require a real full
  name, exact address, phone number, account identifier, financial detail, identity
  document, health detail, or another sensitive fact.
- Record only what is needed to evaluate the sourced lesson outcome and the learning
  hypotheses. A preferred evaluation record contains a research identifier,
  lesson/outcome version, task variant, session outcome, support category, rubric
  ratings, an important-error code, and a brief de-identified rationale.
- Do not retain a full learner utterance, chat transcript, audio, or video by default.
  A short fragment may be retained only when necessary to explain a rating and after
  removing names and other identifying details.
- If raw text, audio, screen, or video capture is proposed for learner research, define
  its purpose, access list, deletion date, withdrawal route, and reuse boundary before
  collection. Obtain explicit informed consent for that capture. No consent means no
  raw capture and must not be treated as lesson failure.
- Do not reuse prototype records for model training, publication, marketing, or an
  unrelated study without a new explicit decision and consent process.
- Redact accidentally supplied sensitive details from review notes. For safety-paused
  sessions, retain only the minimum operational flag and do not place crisis details in
  mastery rationale.

These are product risk controls, not claims from GSE, CEFR, grammar, or vocabulary
sources. The learner-test protocol must later name responsible people and exact
retention periods before collecting participant data.

## Accessible learning materials

- Every required audio or video route needs synchronized captions or a checked
  transcript, replay and pause control, and a readable equivalent for explanations.
- Instructions must be short, readable, and available without relying only on color,
  sound, animation, or memory of a previous screen.
- Permit extra response time, prompt repetition, navigation assistance, and simpler
  non-answer-revealing instructions without treating the learner as unsuccessful.
- Record the support category used so reviewers can interpret evidence consistently;
  do not infer a diagnosis or sensitive trait.

Video remains optional. The gold lesson should separate the learning content from the
medium so its added value can be compared without denying an accessible route.

## Preserving the assessed construct

Support preserves mastery evidence when it improves access without supplying the target
response or replacing the ability being assessed. Examples include extra time for a
speaking task, replay within the authored listening limit, keyboard navigation, or a
plain-language restatement that does not shape the answer.

Support changes the evidence when it replaces the assessed mode or reveals essential
content. Examples include using a transcript to pass a listening outcome or accepting a
written answer as proof of speaking. Record that result as alternate or practice
evidence. It may show learning, but it cannot establish demonstration of the original
outcome.

When an accessible task can preserve the same construct, author it as an equivalent
variant and apply the same rubric. When that is not possible in the prototype, report
the evidence limitation explicitly rather than lowering or silently changing the goal.

## Gold-lesson acceptance checks

Before learner testing, confirm that:

- personal prompts visibly permit fictional answers;
- required media has captions or transcripts and learner controls;
- the mastery brief identifies permitted support and construct-changing support;
- evaluation notes use the minimum de-identified record;
- any raw capture has a consent, access, deletion, and withdrawal plan; and
- early stop or safety pause is not labeled completion or mastery.
