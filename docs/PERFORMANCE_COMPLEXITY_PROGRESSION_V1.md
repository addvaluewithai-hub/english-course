# Performance and Complexity Progression v1

Status: accepted internal curriculum contract.

Each row shows how the same performance dimension changes across levels.

| Dimension | A1 | A2 | B1 | B2 | C1 | C2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Listening input complexity | Recognise familiar words and very basic phrases when delivery is slow, clear, and context-supported. | Understand high-frequency language of immediate relevance and the main point of short clear messages. | Understand main points of clear standard input and relatively clear media on familiar or personally relevant topics. | Follow extended talk and complex argument on reasonably familiar topics and understand most standard-language news and film. | Understand extended discourse with implicit relationships and demanding media without excessive effort. | Understand with ease virtually any kind of live or recorded language, including delivery at fast natural speed. |
| Reading input complexity | Understand familiar names, words, signs, and very simple sentences in predictable material. | Read short simple texts and locate predictable information in everyday materials and personal correspondence. | Understand high-frequency everyday or job-related texts and descriptions of events, feelings, and wishes. | Understand articles and reports with viewpoints, contemporary prose, and relevant technical discussion. | Understand long complex factual, literary, specialised, and technical texts while appreciating style distinctions. | Read with ease virtually all abstract, structurally complex, specialised, and literary written language. |
| Spoken output complexity | Use isolated simple phrases and sentences for personal details, familiar people, places, possessions, and needs. | Use a series of simple phrases and sentences to describe family, background, routines, and immediate surroundings. | Connect phrases to narrate experiences and events and give brief reasons for opinions, hopes, and plans. | Give clear detailed descriptions and explain a viewpoint with advantages, disadvantages, and supporting detail. | Present complex subjects with integrated sub-themes, developed points, and an appropriate conclusion. | Deliver smooth logically structured communication that highlights significant points and fine shades of meaning. |
| Written output complexity | Produce isolated phrases and sentences and complete very simple personal-detail forms or messages. | Produce a series of simple phrases and sentences linked by frequent connectors. | Produce straightforward connected text on familiar or personally interesting topics. | Produce clear detailed texts, essays, and reports that present information or support a position. | Produce clear well-structured detailed texts on complex subjects in a style appropriate to the reader. | Create complex letters, reports, articles, summaries, and reviews in an effective reader-appropriate style. |
| Oral interaction independence | Ask and answer simple questions with repetition, rephrasing, slower speech, and formulation help. | Complete routine tasks and short social exchanges through direct familiar information exchange. | Enter unprepared conversation on familiar topics and handle most common situations while travelling. | Participate actively with useful fluency and spontaneity, sustain views, and manage routine disagreement. | Express ideas fluently and precisely, adapt register, and relate contributions skilfully to others. | Participate effortlessly, use idiomatic language appropriately, and repair so smoothly that difficulty is unobtrusive. |
| Written and online interaction | Post simple greetings or reactions and complete a very simple purchase or personal-details form. | Handle basic social interaction, routine transactions, simple instructions, and supported collaboration. | Interact about experiences and feelings, ask for clarification, and answer comments in some detail. | Collaborate in multi-person tasks and handle misunderstanding when others remain reasonably cooperative. | Handle complex abstract interaction and communication problems through flexible register, clarification, and exemplification. | Use an appropriate tone in virtually any interaction and anticipate cultural, emotional, or communication problems. |
| Mediation complexity | Relay simple predictable information from short signs or notices and show welcome, interest, or understanding. | Convey main points of short simple everyday texts and collaborate on practical tasks with occasional repetition. | Convey familiar information from clear texts, seek simple compromise, and support shared communication. | Relay detailed information and arguments reliably, clarify complex points, and develop or connect others' ideas. | Convey significant ideas from long complex texts and evaluate and connect multiple perspectives. | Reconstruct and evaluate arguments, convey nuance and sociocultural implications, and guide complex discussion sensitively. |
| Language range | Very basic expressions and some one-clause structures for concrete personal information and needs. | Basic patterns and memorised phrases for predictable everyday content, with message compromise outside routine situations. | Sufficient familiar-topic language for some unpredictable situations, with hesitation and circumlocution when limits appear. | Broad general and field-related language, viewpoints, argument, and some complex sentence forms without much restriction. | Broad complex language selected flexibly for clear social, academic, and professional communication. | Comprehensive precise language that differentiates, emphasises, and eliminates ambiguity without visible restriction. |
| Grammatical control | Limited control of a few learnt simple structures and sentence patterns. | Some simple structures are correct, though systematic basic mistakes remain and meaning is usually clear. | Reasonable accuracy in familiar contexts; errors and first-language influence remain but intended meaning is clear. | Relatively high control with occasional slips and some rigid or inaccurate use of complex forms. | Consistently high accuracy; errors are rare and difficult to notice. | Consistent control of complex language even while attention is divided across planning and interaction. |
| Lexical range and control | Basic words and phrases tied to particular concrete situations. | Sufficient for routine transactions, basic communicative needs, and simple survival situations. | Good range for familiar topics and everyday life, with limitations on unfamiliar or complex ideas. | Good general and field-related range with systematic use of many appropriate collocations. | Broad lexical and idiomatic command with effective circumlocution and little obvious searching. | Very broad idiomatic and colloquial repertoire with awareness of connotation and fine meaning distinctions. |
| Pronunciation and intelligibility | A limited learnt repertoire is intelligible with effort and collaborative support. | Generally understandable in familiar situations, though repetition and listener effort may be needed. | Generally intelligible despite regular mispronunciation and noticeable first-language influence. | Intelligible throughout; accent has little effect and prosody generally supports the intended message. | Full phonological range with intelligibility throughout; retained accent does not affect effectiveness. | Precise articulation and prosody enhance subtle meaning; retained accent does not reduce intelligibility or effectiveness. |
| Discourse and cohesion | Link words or groups mainly with very basic linear connectors such as and or then. | Link simple sentences with frequent connectors such as and, but, and because. | Link shorter elements into a connected linear sequence with simple paragraphing and chronological control. | Use varied linking expressions, cohesive devices, supporting detail, and clear logical paragraphs. | Produce smooth, well-structured language with controlled organisational patterns and cohesive devices. | Create fully coherent and cohesive communication using flexible organisational and rhetorical patterns. |
| Fluency | Manage very short pre-packaged utterances with substantial pausing and repair. | Make short understandable contributions with visible hesitation, false starts, and reformulation. | Keep going comprehensibly despite evident pauses for planning and repair. | Sustain longer stretches at a fairly even tempo with few conspicuously long pauses. | Express complex thought almost effortlessly, with only conceptually difficult material affecting flow. | Sustain natural effortless flow, pausing mainly to select the most precise formulation or example. |
| Communication strategy | Signal a basic problem and request simple repetition or clarification with support. | Use simple clarification, repetition, gesture, and basic compensating phrases in routine situations. | Keep going through circumlocution, checking, confirmation, and repair on familiar topics. | Paraphrase, manage turns, and repair most breakdowns while sustaining an independent exchange. | Reformulate flexibly, manage interaction, and resolve ambiguity with little disruption. | Backtrack, restructure, and repair so smoothly that difficulty is unobtrusive. |
| Sociolinguistic appropriateness | Use basic greetings, leave-taking, thanks, and simple politeness formulas. | Use common social conventions and routine polite language in predictable contexts. | Use a wider neutral repertoire and common politeness conventions across familiar contexts. | Select suitable formality and sustain social relationships without significant strain. | Adapt register flexibly and interpret common idiomatic, cultural, and interpersonal signals. | Handle register, connotation, humour, and sociocultural implications with precise sensitivity. |

All 90 stages have CEFR evidence. A1 establishes the first exit boundary;
each later level recycles, consolidates its new boundary, and extends complexity.
