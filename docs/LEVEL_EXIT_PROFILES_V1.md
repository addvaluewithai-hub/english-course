# Level Exit Profiles v1

Status: approved internal curriculum contract.

These are performance boundaries, not lesson counts or grammar/vocabulary checklists.

## A1 - Supported concrete communication

Handle concrete personal details and immediate needs with learnt language and a collaborative partner.

- reception / listening: Recognise familiar words and very basic phrases when delivery is slow, clear, and context-supported.
- reception / reading: Understand familiar names, words, signs, and very simple sentences in predictable material.
- production / speaking: Use isolated simple phrases and sentences for personal details, familiar people, places, possessions, and needs.
- production / writing: Produce isolated phrases and sentences and complete very simple personal-detail forms or messages.
- interaction / oral: Ask and answer simple questions with repetition, rephrasing, slower speech, and formulation help.
- interaction / written online: Post simple greetings or reactions and complete a very simple purchase or personal-details form.
- interaction / mediation: Relay simple predictable information from short signs or notices and show welcome, interest, or understanding.
- language control / range: Very basic expressions and some one-clause structures for concrete personal information and needs.
- language control / grammar: Limited control of a few learnt simple structures and sentence patterns.
- language control / vocabulary: Basic words and phrases tied to particular concrete situations.
- language control / pronunciation: A limited learnt repertoire is intelligible with effort and collaborative support.
- language control / discourse: Link words or groups mainly with very basic linear connectors such as and or then.
- language control / fluency: Manage very short pre-packaged utterances with substantial pausing and repair.

Fresh exit evidence:
- A new concrete personal or immediate-need task
- No copying of a complete model
- Support may clarify but not supply the response

## A2 - Routine direct communication

Complete simple routine exchanges and describe the immediate world through linked simple language.

- reception / listening: Understand high-frequency language of immediate relevance and the main point of short clear messages.
- reception / reading: Read short simple texts and locate predictable information in everyday materials and personal correspondence.
- production / speaking: Use a series of simple phrases and sentences to describe family, background, routines, and immediate surroundings.
- production / writing: Produce a series of simple phrases and sentences linked by frequent connectors.
- interaction / oral: Complete routine tasks and short social exchanges through direct familiar information exchange.
- interaction / written online: Handle basic social interaction, routine transactions, simple instructions, and supported collaboration.
- interaction / mediation: Convey main points of short simple everyday texts and collaborate on practical tasks with occasional repetition.
- language control / range: Basic patterns and memorised phrases for predictable everyday content, with message compromise outside routine situations.
- language control / grammar: Some simple structures are correct, though systematic basic mistakes remain and meaning is usually clear.
- language control / vocabulary: Sufficient for routine transactions, basic communicative needs, and simple survival situations.
- language control / pronunciation: Generally understandable in familiar situations, though repetition and listener effort may be needed.
- language control / discourse: Link simple sentences with frequent connectors such as and, but, and because.
- language control / fluency: Make short understandable contributions with visible hesitation, false starts, and reformulation.

Fresh exit evidence:
- A new routine transaction
- A short linked description
- Successful repair after a bounded misunderstanding

## B1 - Connected familiar independence

Operate independently in familiar life, travel, work, and personal-interest situations using connected language.

- reception / listening: Understand main points of clear standard input and relatively clear media on familiar or personally relevant topics.
- reception / reading: Understand high-frequency everyday or job-related texts and descriptions of events, feelings, and wishes.
- production / speaking: Connect phrases to narrate experiences and events and give brief reasons for opinions, hopes, and plans.
- production / writing: Produce straightforward connected text on familiar or personally interesting topics.
- interaction / oral: Enter unprepared conversation on familiar topics and handle most common situations while travelling.
- interaction / written online: Interact about experiences and feelings, ask for clarification, and answer comments in some detail.
- interaction / mediation: Convey familiar information from clear texts, seek simple compromise, and support shared communication.
- language control / range: Sufficient familiar-topic language for some unpredictable situations, with hesitation and circumlocution when limits appear.
- language control / grammar: Reasonable accuracy in familiar contexts; errors and first-language influence remain but intended meaning is clear.
- language control / vocabulary: Good range for familiar topics and everyday life, with limitations on unfamiliar or complex ideas.
- language control / pronunciation: Generally intelligible despite regular mispronunciation and noticeable first-language influence.
- language control / discourse: Link shorter elements into a connected linear sequence with simple paragraphing and chronological control.
- language control / fluency: Keep going comprehensibly despite evident pauses for planning and repair.

Fresh exit evidence:
- An unscripted familiar problem
- A connected narrative
- A reasoned response in more than one context

## B2 - Detailed independent communication

Understand and produce clear detailed language, sustain viewpoints, and interact with useful spontaneity.

- reception / listening: Follow extended talk and complex argument on reasonably familiar topics and understand most standard-language news and film.
- reception / reading: Understand articles and reports with viewpoints, contemporary prose, and relevant technical discussion.
- production / speaking: Give clear detailed descriptions and explain a viewpoint with advantages, disadvantages, and supporting detail.
- production / writing: Produce clear detailed texts, essays, and reports that present information or support a position.
- interaction / oral: Participate actively with useful fluency and spontaneity, sustain views, and manage routine disagreement.
- interaction / written online: Collaborate in multi-person tasks and handle misunderstanding when others remain reasonably cooperative.
- interaction / mediation: Relay detailed information and arguments reliably, clarify complex points, and develop or connect others' ideas.
- language control / range: Broad general and field-related language, viewpoints, argument, and some complex sentence forms without much restriction.
- language control / grammar: Relatively high control with occasional slips and some rigid or inaccurate use of complex forms.
- language control / vocabulary: Good general and field-related range with systematic use of many appropriate collocations.
- language control / pronunciation: Intelligible throughout; accent has little effect and prosody generally supports the intended message.
- language control / discourse: Use varied linking expressions, cohesive devices, supporting detail, and clear logical paragraphs.
- language control / fluency: Sustain longer stretches at a fairly even tempo with few conspicuously long pauses.

Fresh exit evidence:
- Interpretation of complex input
- Spontaneous contribution
- A supported viewpoint for a new audience and topic

## C1 - Flexible complex communication

Use English flexibly for complex social, academic, and professional purposes, including implicit meaning.

- reception / listening: Understand extended discourse with implicit relationships and demanding media without excessive effort.
- reception / reading: Understand long complex factual, literary, specialised, and technical texts while appreciating style distinctions.
- production / speaking: Present complex subjects with integrated sub-themes, developed points, and an appropriate conclusion.
- production / writing: Produce clear well-structured detailed texts on complex subjects in a style appropriate to the reader.
- interaction / oral: Express ideas fluently and precisely, adapt register, and relate contributions skilfully to others.
- interaction / written online: Handle complex abstract interaction and communication problems through flexible register, clarification, and exemplification.
- interaction / mediation: Convey significant ideas from long complex texts and evaluate and connect multiple perspectives.
- language control / range: Broad complex language selected flexibly for clear social, academic, and professional communication.
- language control / grammar: Consistently high accuracy; errors are rare and difficult to notice.
- language control / vocabulary: Broad lexical and idiomatic command with effective circumlocution and little obvious searching.
- language control / pronunciation: Full phonological range with intelligibility throughout; retained accent does not affect effectiveness.
- language control / discourse: Produce smooth, well-structured language with controlled organisational patterns and cohesive devices.
- language control / fluency: Express complex thought almost effortlessly, with only conceptually difficult material affecting flow.

Fresh exit evidence:
- Synthesis of demanding input
- A complex position in multiple domains
- Audience and register adaptation without rehearsed wording

## C2 - Precise nuanced synthesis

Synthesize complex sources and communicate precise nuances with consistently appropriate language.

- reception / listening: Understand with ease virtually any kind of live or recorded language, including delivery at fast natural speed.
- reception / reading: Read with ease virtually all abstract, structurally complex, specialised, and literary written language.
- production / speaking: Deliver smooth logically structured communication that highlights significant points and fine shades of meaning.
- production / writing: Create complex letters, reports, articles, summaries, and reviews in an effective reader-appropriate style.
- interaction / oral: Participate effortlessly, use idiomatic language appropriately, and repair so smoothly that difficulty is unobtrusive.
- interaction / written online: Use an appropriate tone in virtually any interaction and anticipate cultural, emotional, or communication problems.
- interaction / mediation: Reconstruct and evaluate arguments, convey nuance and sociocultural implications, and guide complex discussion sensitively.
- language control / range: Comprehensive precise language that differentiates, emphasises, and eliminates ambiguity without visible restriction.
- language control / grammar: Consistent control of complex language even while attention is divided across planning and interaction.
- language control / vocabulary: Very broad idiomatic and colloquial repertoire with awareness of connotation and fine meaning distinctions.
- language control / pronunciation: Precise articulation and prosody enhance subtle meaning; retained accent does not reduce intelligibility or effectiveness.
- language control / discourse: Create fully coherent and cohesive communication using flexible organisational and rhetorical patterns.
- language control / fluency: Sustain natural effortless flow, pausing mainly to select the most precise formulation or example.

Fresh exit evidence:
- Synthesis of unfamiliar complex sources
- Precise nuance under realistic cognitive load
- Audience, register, and sociocultural appropriateness
