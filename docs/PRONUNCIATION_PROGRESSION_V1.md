# Pronunciation Progression v1

Status: accepted internal curriculum contract.

The goal is intelligible, effective communication—not accent removal. General American
is the teaching reference for models and IPA; a retained learner accent remains acceptable.

| Strand | Guided production begins | Family consolidation | A1 | C2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sound perception and word recognition | A1 | A2 | Distinguish selected high-value sounds, word boundaries, names, numbers, and learnt chunks in very clear speech. | Interpret fine phonological detail, marked delivery, and subtle prosodic meaning across demanding input. |
| Consonant contrasts and place or manner clarity | A1 | B1 | Produce selected high-impact consonant contrasts clearly enough in names, numbers, key nouns, verbs, and chunks. | Use precise articulation deliberately where it supports subtle contrast, emphasis, or rhetorical effect. |
| Vowel contrasts and reduced vowels | A1 | B1 | Produce selected meaning-changing vowel contrasts in a bounded learnt repertoire with modelling and repair. | Adjust vowel and reduction detail precisely when it contributes to nuance, contrast, or performance effect. |
| Syllable shape, consonant clusters, and word endings | A1 | A2 | Preserve selected final consonants and simple clusters when they carry essential word identity or meaning. | Use precise syllable shaping and deliberate simplification only when context and style make it appropriate. |
| Audible grammatical endings | A1 | B1 | Make selected plural, possessive, and simple verb endings audible when omission changes immediate meaning. | Use grammatical sound detail precisely while varying reduction appropriately for style and emphasis. |
| Word stress and stress-sensitive recognition | A1 | B1 | Place recognisable stress in selected names, numbers, nationalities, jobs, and other lesson-critical words. | Manipulate normal and contrastive lexical stress precisely for meaning and stylistic effect. |
| Sentence prominence and rhythm | A2 | B2 | Notice and imitate prominence inside short learnt chunks without a natural-rhythm mastery demand. | Shape rhythm and prominence precisely for subtle meaning, rhetorical design, and interpersonal effect. |
| Connected-speech perception and production | A2 | B2 | Recognise a small set of frequent linked or reduced forms inside familiar, very clear chunks. | Use or resist connected-speech processes deliberately for precision, clarity, style, and audience. |
| Intonation, interaction, and stance | A2 | B2 | Use recognisable contours in a few learnt greetings, questions, checks, and polite responses. | Use fine intonational choices precisely for implication, humour, tact, ambiguity, and rhetorical effect. |
| Chunking, pausing, monitoring, and pronunciation repair | A1 | B1 | Deliver short learnt sense groups, pause safely, and repeat or spell a key item when not understood. | Backtrack, rechunk, and adjust articulation or prosody so smoothly that difficulty is unobtrusive. |

## Arabic-speaking learner risks

The 12 risk entries are hypotheses, not facts about every Arabic speaker. Each requires
dialect-aware specialist review and learner observation before it may drive a target.

Mastery is demonstrated in fresh communication. Isolated imitation is diagnostic only,
and no assessment may penalise an accent that preserves meaning and intelligibility.
