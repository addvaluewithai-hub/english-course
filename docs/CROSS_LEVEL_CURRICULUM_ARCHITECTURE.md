# Cross-Level Curriculum Architecture v0

Status: owner-requested proposal for curriculum-team review.

## Decision

Design the product vertically from A1 through C2 before mass-producing the remaining
A1 lessons. Use Pre-A1 as an onboarding and support band, not as a promised exit level.
Use the GSE plus bands (A2+, B1+, B2+) as internal progress milestones rather than new
CEFR exit claims.

The gold lesson and Unit 1 are authored prototypes, not fixed curriculum constraints.
They may be kept, revised, replaced, or retired after reconciliation. A small delivery
pilot may continue, but full-A1 production is blocked until the A1 map is reconciled
with the approved vertical spine.

## What this architecture controls

The spine answers four questions for every ability or language item:

1. When may the learner first encounter it?
2. When is supported use expected?
3. When is independent control required?
4. Where is it recycled, consolidated, and extended?

It does not prescribe lesson transcripts, screen order, exact lesson counts, or media.
Those are level-specific delivery decisions made after the curriculum contract is
approved.

## Supported progression

| Band | Product role | Broad change in learner control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-A1 | Diagnostic on-ramp | Recognise and use isolated words and formulaic expressions with strong support. |
| A1 | Exit level | Handle concrete personal details and immediate needs with learnt language and a collaborative partner. |
| A2 | Exit level | Complete simple routine exchanges and describe the immediate world in linked simple language. |
| B1 | Exit level | Operate independently in familiar life, travel, work, and personal-interest situations using connected language. |
| B2 | Exit level | Understand and produce clear, detailed language, sustain viewpoints, and interact with useful spontaneity. |
| C1 | Exit level | Use English flexibly for complex social, academic, and professional purposes, including implicit meaning. |
| C2 | Exit level | Synthesize complex sources and communicate precise nuances with consistently appropriate language. |

## Required vertical strands

Every level plan must cover these strands together:

- communicative abilities and functions;
- listening and reading input conditions and complexity;
- speaking and writing output length, organisation, and independence;
- oral, written, online, and collaborative interaction;
- text, concept, and communication mediation;
- grammar, sentence patterns, and discourse organisation;
- vocabulary, chunks, collocations, register, and domain language;
- pronunciation, sound articulation, stress, rhythm, intonation, and intelligibility;
- repair, clarification, compensation, and learning strategies;
- fresh performance and delayed-retention evidence.

## Lifecycle contract

Use the following ordered states:

`expose -> introduce -> practise -> recycle -> consolidate -> extend`

- `expose`: comprehension is useful; production is not required.
- `introduce`: meaning and form are made noticeable with strong support.
- `practise`: the learner produces the item in controlled or guided tasks.
- `recycle`: the item returns in a new outcome or context.
- `consolidate`: independent control is required in fresh performance.
- `extend`: range, nuance, register, or structural complexity grows.

An item can be exposed above or below the level where independent control is required.
Receptive and productive expectations must therefore be stored separately.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. CEFR Companion Volume defines broad ability, activity, competence, and qualitative
   level boundaries.
2. Pearson GSE Adult objectives provide granular, scored communicative targets and
   internal progress points.
3. English Grammar Profile provides empirical English grammar features by CEFR level.
4. Pearson GSE Grammar provides a secondary productive-grammar cross-check.
5. Oxford 3000 and 5000 provide levelled lexical pools, not a mandatory lesson order.
6. Arabic-speaker risks, examples, and teaching choices are product hypotheses until
   reviewed by qualified language specialists and tested with learners.

The source field and the product-decision field must never be combined. A CEFR or GSE
level is evidence; the lesson, unit, lifecycle owner, and assessment design are product
decisions.

## Vocabulary policy

Do not teach a word merely because it appears in a levelled list. Select vocabulary
that serves an approved communicative outcome, then audit the whole level for breadth,
frequency, recycling, chunks, collocations, and deliberate omissions.

Oxford 3000 covers A1-B2 and Oxford 5000 adds B2-C1 vocabulary. Neither source provides
a C2 lexical checklist. C2 therefore needs a documented corpus- and domain-informed
method, reviewed separately, rather than an invented word count.

## Grammar policy

Do not convert all 1,222 EGP features into lessons. Each feature must receive a
disposition: productive target, receptive exposure, supporting evidence, later-level
extension, optional enrichment, duplicate/overlap, or excluded with rationale.

The level of an EGP feature is not automatically the lesson in which it is first seen.
The spine decides ownership and lifecycle after communicative need, prerequisites,
frequency, and cognitive load are considered.

## Pronunciation policy

The target is intelligibility and effective meaning, not accent removal. Each level
must progress sound control and prosody while retaining space for a learner's accent.
The CEFR phonology grid is the level boundary; an English-specific teaching sequence
still needs to be authored and reviewed for Arabic-speaking learners.

## Approval boundary

This document and `docs/LEVEL_EXIT_PROFILES_V0.md` are a proposal, not a completed
cross-level mapping. They may guide source normalization and review, but they cannot be
used to claim that every CEFR, GSE, grammar, or lexical record has already been placed.

Mass A1 production may resume only after:

- all-level source normalization and disposition audits pass;
- level exit profiles are approved;
- progression strands and lifecycle ownership are reviewed;
- the 81 current A1 candidates and Unit 1 are reconciled without a fixed-count bias;
- A1 scope, sequence, unit boundaries, and assessment coverage are locked as v1.
