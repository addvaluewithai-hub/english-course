# Mastery and Progress Policy v1

Status: locked for the learning-model phase. Gold-lesson and learner-test evidence may
revise thresholds through a recorded decision.

## Purpose and scope

This policy defines observable lesson mastery before any app, database, or scoring
system exists. It applies to every lesson-sized ability, but each lesson must replace
the generic language below with evidence tied to its own sourced outcome.

Completion, demonstration, and retention are different claims:

- **Completion** records that the required lesson experience ended. It does not prove
  the learner can perform the ability.
- **Demonstration** means the learner met the lesson rubric in a fresh task without a
  complete visible model or answer-revealing help.
- **Retention** means the learner demonstrated the ability again after a delay in a
  different fresh task.

Watching, viewing the toolkit, finishing practice, or earning a recognition-item score
may be useful learning evidence, but none can independently establish demonstration.

## Conditions for mastery evidence

A mastery attempt is valid only when all of these conditions hold:

1. It tests the stated lesson outcome, not an easier proxy skill.
2. The situation or details are meaningfully different from guided examples while
   remaining at the taught level.
3. A complete model answer is hidden, and the learner must supply the essential
   language, interpretation, or action.
4. Instructions, neutral repetition, and accessibility support may be available, but
   they must not reveal the target response.
5. The evaluator records performance against every lesson-required rubric dimension.

Accessibility support must also preserve the assessed construct. If support changes a
speaking outcome into writing or reveals listening content through a transcript, the
result is alternate or practice evidence rather than demonstration of the original
outcome. `docs/PRIVACY_ACCESSIBILITY_POLICY.md` defines the current boundary.

The attempt must contain enough evidence for the outcome. One isolated correct answer
does not pass an outcome that requires a sequence, interaction, or sustained response.

A sentence to copy, a translated target answer, answer choices that expose the full
response, or corrective prompting during the attempt makes that attempt supported
practice. After feedback, the learner may try a new fresh variant for demonstration.

## Reusable three-level rubric

Every lesson marks each dimension as required or not applicable and adds concrete,
outcome-specific evidence. Comprehension and outcome performance are always required;
interaction or transfer and control or repair are required wherever the outcome makes
them observable.

| Dimension | 0 - Not yet | 1 - Supported or partial | 2 - Independent and adequate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Comprehension | Misunderstands the essential prompt, input, or situation. | Understands only after answer-shaping rephrasing or repeated cues. | Interprets the essential prompt, input, or situation with no answer-revealing help. |
| Outcome performance | Does not complete the stated action, or only copies a model. | Completes part of the action or depends on stems, choices, or corrective cues. | Completes the stated action with the essential meaning and content, producing English when the outcome requires it. |
| Interaction or transfer | Cannot respond, continue, or adapt when the context changes. | Responds or adapts only with scaffolding that shapes the answer. | Responds appropriately or transfers the ability to the fresh variant. |
| Control or repair | Target-language problems prevent the required meaning or response. | Meaning is partly clear, but an important target issue remains after support. | Target language is intelligible and appropriate enough for A1; when an important issue is surfaced, the learner can repair it or briefly explain the pattern. |

`Demonstrated` requires a level 2 on every required dimension in the same valid fresh
attempt. Do not average scores: strength in one dimension cannot hide a critical gap in
another. Accent, harmless hesitation, and non-target errors do not block demonstration
when meaning and the lesson outcome are achieved.

For the current gold-lesson test, demonstration must be determinable from the fresh
performance itself. A post-performance teach-back is recorded separately: it may
corroborate control or repair or reveal a review need, but it cannot supply a missing
required rating, reverse a failed performance, or replace the communicative outcome.
Recognition questions can diagnose comprehension or weak items but cannot substitute
for production, interaction, or transfer required by the outcome.

## Progress states and transitions

- **Not started:** no meaningful engagement with the lesson ability.
- **Learning:** the learner has begun input, explanation, or supported noticing.
- **Practiced:** the learner has attempted the ability in guided or personalized
  practice, but no valid mastery pass is recorded.
- **Demonstrated:** the most recent valid mastery attempt met every required criterion.
- **Needs review:** later evidence shows a previously demonstrated ability is weak, or
  an important recurring gap requires targeted review before another mastery attempt.
- **Retained:** after demonstration and a delay, a new valid task again meets every
  required criterion.

The normal path is `Not started -> Learning -> Practiced -> Demonstrated -> Retained`.
`Needs review` is a recovery state, not a mandatory step or punishment. It may follow a
failed delayed probe or later performance evidence from `Demonstrated` or `Retained`.
Targeted review returns the learner to practice; only a new valid attempt can restore
`Demonstrated` or `Retained`.

Completing lesson screens may coexist with `Learning`, `Practiced`, `Demonstrated`, or
`Needs review`. Completion must never advance the mastery state automatically. A failed
first mastery attempt normally leaves the learner `Practiced`; use `Needs review` when
there is prior mastery evidence or a specifically identified recurring weakness.

## Minimum lesson-specific mastery specification

Before a lesson can pass content review, record:

- the measurable outcome and its source-objective links;
- a fresh mastery-task family: a primary brief plus at least one equivalent replacement
  variant or bounded mutation rules, with the maximum attempts stated;
- concrete level-2 evidence for each required dimension;
- critical failures and non-target errors that should not block success;
- permitted instructions, accessibility support, and disallowed answer-revealing help;
- the weak item or pattern to review after each likely failure;
- a distinct delayed-probe brief for retention.

For every evaluated attempt, retain the minimum structured evidence defined in
`docs/PRIVACY_ACCESSIBILITY_POLICY.md`: lesson/outcome version, task variant, time,
support category, dimension ratings, important error or repair code, and a short
de-identified rationale. Raw utterance storage is not required. This remains an
evidence requirement, not a production data-model decision.

## Testing hypotheses and deferred rules

The exact retention interval, number of successful probes, task-variant difficulty,
and level of evaluator agreement are not yet evidence-backed. Set initial hypotheses
when the gold lesson and learner-test protocol are designed, then revise this policy
from observed performance. Do not silently convert those hypotheses into permanent
scoring or scheduling rules.

## Basis in current project evidence

- The product brief rejects video watching, exercise completion, and an 8/10
  multiple-choice result as sufficient mastery, and requires a small success rubric
  (`references/legacy/conversation between me and ai.txt`, lines 1713-1724).
- Its example requires understanding, personal production, interaction, independence,
  and repair or explanation, then proposes the six progress states used here (same
  file, lines 1726-1749).
- The live-teacher brief requires a fresh final conversation led independently by the
  learner and says not to stop after one successful answer
  (`references/legacy/live ai agent.txt`, lines 143-158).
- That brief also treats retry after one important correction as learning behavior and
  excludes harmless hesitation from correction (same file, lines 79-86 and 196-204).

These legacy inputs establish product intent, not curriculum-level claims. Lesson
criteria must still inherit their actual language level and content from traceable
curriculum sources in later phases.
