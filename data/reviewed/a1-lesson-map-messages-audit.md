## Candidate decisions

- Everyday visually supported signs, notices, and instructions form one bounded
  reading-and-action outcome.
- The CEFR audio-visual descriptor is split into named partial claims because
  identifying a headline-news topic and identifying an advertised product need
  separate fresh-task judgments.
- Understanding an incoming postcard remains distinct from writing one. Postcard
  writing extends the existing friend-message ability as a format variant rather than
  becoming a tiny duplicate candidate.
- The online task is one standard two-turn exchange: the fresh task requires both an
  initial positive or negative reaction and a context-appropriate thanks or apology.
- Conveying selected practical information from a public text remains distinct from
  roughly translating its words or phrases. Both tasks declare Language A and Language
  B and make no source-level equivalence claim.
