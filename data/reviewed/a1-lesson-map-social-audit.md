## Candidate decisions

- Agreeing to a request and accepting an offer remain separate candidates because the
  two responses can be mastered independently.
- Greeting, asking how someone is, reacting to simple news, and leave-taking form one
  bounded social exchange. Making an introduction remains with the existing
  introduce-another-person candidate; responding when introduced is a separate turn.
- Addressing an audience from a very short prepared text is distinct from an
  unrehearsed interpersonal introduction because the permitted support and performance
  setting differ.
- Please, thank you, and sorry are treated as one small sociolinguistic form-to-purpose
  set, while greeting, farewell, and introduction clauses remain named partials in
  their own social tasks.
- A response to a creative work preserves the CEFR channel ambiguity by declaring one
  configured spoken or written channel per fresh task, not requiring both.
- Inviting contributions and checking understanding, showing interest in an idea,
  sharing an idea and asking what others think, and showing sympathy each retain a
  separate mastery judgment.
