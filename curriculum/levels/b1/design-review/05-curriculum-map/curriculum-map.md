# B1 Curriculum Map — Manual Stage-05 Skeleton

Status: **STAGE 05 COMPLETE — BASELINE MAP FOR EXACT LEDGER; NOT A COMMERCIAL/FIXED LESSON COUNT**  
Level: **B1**  
Reviewed architecture: **20 capability families + 8 cross-cutting requirements**  
Baseline shape emerging from manual mapping: **12 units / 84 lesson slots**

This map was built manually after reviewing all B1 abilities, phrases, grammar systems, lexical/content homes, pronunciation, B1 exit requirements and locked A2 carry-forward. It does **not** copy A1’s 10/62 or A2’s 12/83 shape, and no target lesson count was supplied before mapping.

The **84-slot** baseline emerged from separating the B1 performance boundaries that need independent introduction/practice/evidence while preserving unit-end fresh retrieval and the three explicit B1 exit requirements. Stage 06 exact source-record/load/lifecycle evidence may still move, merge or split slots.

## Shape accounting

- **12 units**
- **84 lesson slots total**
- **68 instructional / focused-retrieval slots**
- **11 unit-end fresh-transfer slots**
- **5 final integration / exit-evidence slots**
- unit sizes vary from **5 to 9** lessons because capability complexity and dependency load vary

## Mapping rules

1. One primary communicative/evidence outcome per instructional lesson; coherent integrated skills are allowed when they serve that outcome.
2. A2 language is prerequisite/retrieval, not relabelled as new B1 inventory.
3. Grammar systems serve performance; no one-EGP-row-one-lesson mapping.
4. Broad lexical domains are contextual content, not unit-defining capability claims.
5. C10 familiar-topic description/explanation is deliberately distributed; there is no giant “familiar topics” unit.
6. C08 repair/circumlocution, X02/X03 connected discourse, X04/X05 interaction, X08 reference-building and pronunciation recur across units.
7. Every designated transfer/final integration slot has **zero planned new required source introductions**. Stage 06 must enforce this exactly.
8. The three B1 product-policy requirements—unscripted familiar problem, connected narrative, reasoned response in more than one context—receive spaced evidence before final integration.
9. Final mastery evidence must change context/prompt and reduce model dependence; it cannot be copied from a complete model.
10. If Stage 06 exact load or lifecycle evidence contradicts this skeleton, reopen the map rather than hide content.

# Unit 1 — From Routine Exchange to Independent Conversation
**7 slots**  
Primary capabilities: **C06, C08, C15**.  
Cross-cutting: **X01, X04, X05**.  
Pronunciation emphasis: connected-speech perception, intonation/stance, chunking/repair.  
A2 carry-forward: social routines + bounded repair + simple survey/interview.

Purpose: move immediately beyond A2 scripted/routine exchange toward B1 unprepared familiar conversation, active listening, follow-up, repair and lexical-gap management.

### U1-L01 — Reconnect without a script
Enter a familiar conversation from an unpredictable but familiar opening, retrieve A2 social language, respond naturally and establish a topic. No re-teaching of greetings/wellbeing as new B1 content.

### U1-L02 — Keep a familiar conversation going
Sustain several turns through active listening, follow-up, topic continuation and simple turn management; use X05 rather than relying on teacher-supplied next questions.

### U1-L03 — Share personal updates, feelings and reactions
Exchange recent/familiar personal news and feelings, respond with appropriate interest/stance and extend beyond isolated answers.

### U1-L04 — Paraphrase, clarify and work around a missing word
Use C08: ask for clarification, gloss/rephrase, confirm meaning and use circumlocution when exact vocabulary is unavailable.

### U1-L05 — Understand and respond in an informal interview
Follow a short familiar interview, identify the main information and respond to prepared questions without turning the task into scripted Q&A.

### U1-L06 — Ask follow-up questions and check information
Take some initiative in a simple interview: spontaneous follow-up, reformulated question, repetition/confirmation and accurate information checking.

### U1-L07 — Fresh conversation/interview transfer
New person/topic card with no complete script: enter conversation → sustain it → ask follow-up → resolve one lexical/meaning gap → close. **Zero planned new required content.**

**Unit critic:** B1 independence begins here; C08 is performance repair, not a phrase list; interview follow-up is kept distinct from generic conversation. No split/merge required before exact load review.

# Unit 2 — Work, Study, Goals, Plans, and Personal Development
**7 slots**  
Primary capabilities: **C10, C15, C17, C20**; cross-link C02.  
Major lexical homes: **L01, L06, L24** plus reviewed general breadth.  
Pronunciation: word stress, grammatical endings, chunking connected explanations.  
A2 carry-forward: background/profile exchange, simple skills/work/study facts, A2 plans/intentions.

Purpose: turn A2 personal facts into connected B1 explanation about work/study, achievements, responsibilities, goals and plans, with forms/application/interview evidence.

### U2-L01 — Describe current work/study and responsibilities
Give/understand a connected account of current work/study role, duties and routine demands using richer reference rather than isolated job labels.

### U2-L02 — Talk about qualifications, achievements and skills
Explain completed/current qualifications, achievements and abilities; retrieve A2 skills language and extend it with B1 education/work lexis.

### U2-L03 — Explain goals, ambitions, intentions and plans
Connect present situation to future goal/plan; use B1 intention/prediction resources only where the learner performance needs them.

### U2-L04 — Complete a detailed application or education/work form
C20 structured-information evidence: identify fields, provide accurate relevant details and distinguish form completion from free writing.

### U2-L05 — Explain progress, change and current development
Describe how a familiar work/study skill or situation has changed, using comparison/current-relevance language without turning the lesson into a present-perfect chapter.

### U2-L06 — Handle a course/job-style familiar interview
Integrate background, qualification, goals and follow-up/checking. Keep B1 scope familiar and non-specialist; no high-stakes professional-interview claim.

### U2-L07 — Fresh profile/application transfer
New course/work scenario: read requirements → complete key form fields → take part in short interview → give a connected profile/goal response. **Zero planned new required content.**

**Unit critic:** forms, connected explanation and interview remain separate success conditions; future language is function-led; work/education vocabulary does not become a topic dump.

# Unit 3 — Opinions, Choices, Advice, and Practical Solutions
**8 slots**  
Primary capabilities: **C07, C08**; receptive cross-link **C05**.  
Cross-cutting: **X02, X03, X04, X05**.  
Major lexical homes: L03, L08, L21, L22, L23.  
Grammar emphasis: comparison, stance/hedging, advice/possibility, real conditions, bounded imagined alternatives.  
Exit path: first major **reasoned-response** evidence.

Purpose: progress from A2 simple opinion/suggestion/advice to B1 reasoned alternatives, polite disagreement, consequences and collaborative problem solving.

### U3-L01 — State a viewpoint and give a clear reason
Express a familiar-topic opinion, foreground the main point and support it with one or more linked reasons/examples.

### U3-L02 — Compare options and explain a choice
Compare advantages/disadvantages, quality/amount/trend or practical features and select an option for an explicit reason.

### U3-L03 — Agree, disagree and hedge appropriately
Respond to another viewpoint with support, partial agreement, polite disagreement, certainty/possibility and appropriate stance.

### U3-L04 — Give advice and make suggestions with reasons
Move beyond fixed A2 advice/suggestion routines: recommend an action, explain why and respond to an alternative.

### U3-L05 — Discuss real conditions and likely consequences
Use real/future condition-result language to discuss practical choices, warnings or outcomes. Keep it separate from imagined/counterfactual G14.

### U3-L06 — Discuss imagined alternatives and `If I were you` advice
Use a bounded imagined-situation repertoire for advice/alternative choice. Do not use this slot to force full counterfactual/regret mastery before Stage-8 role review.

### U3-L07 — Negotiate a simple solution or compromise
Two people with partly different priorities compare options, clarify, modify proposals and reach a workable familiar solution.

### U3-L08 — Fresh reasoned-response transfer — Context 1
New familiar issue/decision: interpret short input → state viewpoint → compare alternatives → give reasons → respond to a challenge. **Zero planned new required content.**

**Unit critic:** C07 remains a communicative performance, not a modal/conditional chapter; the first reasoned-response evidence is intentionally not the final one required by the exit contract.

# Unit 4 — Experiences, Stories, Background, and Change
**9 slots**  
Primary capabilities: **C04, C09, C18**.  
Cross-cutting: **X02, X03, X08**.  
Major lexical homes: L02, L04, L19/L20 as optional story contexts.  
Grammar emphasis: expanded past simple, past continuous, past perfect, `used to`, present perfect simple/continuous, narrative cohesion.  
Exit path: connected narrative.

Purpose: build a real B1 narrative system from A2 factual past/simple sequence without teaching a tense table.

### U4-L01 — Follow the storyline and event sequence
Understand a clear familiar narrative, identify main events, sequence, likely development and key reactions; production is not yet the success condition.

### U4-L02 — Tell a connected sequence of past events
Retrieve A2 past language, extend verb/adverb range and link events into a coherent sequence with chronology.

### U4-L03 — Set background and main events
Use past continuous + past simple functionally to distinguish ongoing background/scene from main events.

### U4-L04 — Add earlier background with past perfect
Show that one relevant event/state happened before another past point; keep prior-time meaning visible rather than presenting a form table.

### U4-L05 — Connect experience to now
Use present perfect simple/continuous for duration, recent/current relevance and continuing/repeated activity, building from locked A2 experience use rather than restarting the system.

### U4-L06 — Compare past habits/states with now
Use `used to` and related comparison language to describe change over time in a personal/familiar context.

### U4-L07 — Add reactions, outcomes and perspective
Use narrative result/reaction language (`turn out`, `end up`, stance/reaction resources) and reference/cohesion to make a story meaningful rather than merely chronological.

### U4-L08 — Write a connected familiar narrative
Plan, paragraph, sequence and revise a straightforward narrative; X03/X07/X08 matter as text-quality conditions.

### U4-L09 — Fresh connected-narrative evidence
Unseen prompt/picture/experience frame: understand minimal context → produce a connected narrative with background, sequence and outcome; no complete model. **Zero planned new required content.**

**Unit critic:** reception and production remain distinct; past systems are sequenced by narrative function; U4-L09 supplies the first explicit connected-narrative mastery path before final integration.

# Unit 5 — Practical Information, Media, and Viewpoints
**7 slots**  
Primary capabilities: **C01, C02, C05**.  
Cross-cutting: **X01, X02, X06, X08**.  
Lexical breadth: media/public/science/environment/culture/business can appear as familiar content without becoming topic capabilities.  
Pronunciation: connected-speech perception and prominence recognition.

Purpose: extend A2 short practical information into B1 clear factual media/talks and straightforward viewpoint/logic comprehension.

### U5-L01 — Find needed information and follow practical instructions
Search everyday/work material efficiently, locate relevant details and follow a straightforward procedure/instruction without reading every word equally.

### U5-L02 — Understand a factual article or web text
Identify main point, important supporting detail and information structure in a clear familiar factual text.

### U5-L03 — Understand a clear talk, podcast, class or training input
Follow main points and useful details in extended but familiar spoken input, using repetition/context support where the B1 source boundary permits it.

### U5-L04 — Understand a factual media/news item
Identify what happened/what matters in relatively clear familiar media and separate core information from supporting detail.

### U5-L05 — Distinguish fact, opinion, viewpoint and attitude
Recognise speaker/writer stance and distinguish it from factual information; do not replace receptive evidence with learner opinion production.

### U5-L06 — Follow cause/effect, problem/solution and line of argument
Track straightforward logical relationships, examples and conclusions using X02/X03 signals.

### U5-L07 — Fresh information/viewpoint transfer
New mixed practical/media sources: locate required facts, identify main viewpoint/logical relation and justify an information choice from the source. **Zero planned new required content.**

**Unit critic:** targeted search, factual comprehension and viewpoint interpretation remain separately assessable; broad topical lexicon stays contextual/receptive where appropriate.

# Unit 6 — Summarising, Reporting, Comparing, and Mediating Information
**7 slots**  
Primary capability: **C16**; cross-links C05/C07/C17.  
Cross-cutting: X02, X03, X08.  
Lexical homes: L05, L18, L22, L23 plus broad factual content.  
Grammar emphasis: reported statements/questions/requests, reporting verbs, source comparison/reference.  
Exit path: B1 mediation.

Purpose: transform information from clear familiar sources for another purpose/person; copying source sentences is not mediation.

### U6-L01 — Select key information for another purpose
Identify which facts/main points are relevant to a new question/person and deliberately leave out non-essential detail.

### U6-L02 — Summarise a short article, talk or diagram
Produce a short accurate summary with main/supporting information hierarchy rather than sentence-by-sentence copying.

### U6-L03 — Compare information from two clear sources
Identify similarities/differences, reconcile straightforward facts and report the comparison in connected language.

### U6-L04 — Report statements, questions, requests and instructions
Use reported-speech resources to relay useful information accurately; grammar serves the relay task rather than becoming a transformation exercise chapter.

### U6-L05 — Report a discussion or another person’s viewpoint
Convey key opinions/reasons from a familiar exchange while keeping source vs reporter stance clear.

### U6-L06 — Collate information and support a simple compromise
Combine clear source facts/preferences, explain trade-offs and help participants reach a practical shared option.

### U6-L07 — Fresh mediation mission
New source set + recipient need: select → compare/summarise → relay → answer a clarification question. **Zero planned new required content.**

**Unit critic:** C16 is source transformation, not comprehension; reported speech remains embedded infrastructure; factual accuracy and relevance outrank grammatical display.

Primary capability: **C12**; cross-links C01/C08/C17/C20.  
Lexical homes: L07, L13, L25.  
A2 carry-forward: directions/tickets/reservations are retrieved, not restarted.

Purpose: move from predictable A2 travel transactions toward less-routine B1 travel changes/problems while preserving practical outcome and repair.

### U7-L01 — Give and follow more detailed directions
Handle multi-step route/location language, landmarks and clarification while maintaining a usable route sequence.

### U7-L02 — Understand transport information, delays and alternatives
Extract time/status/platform/route changes and compare practical transport options from clear announcements/timetables/messages.

### U7-L03 — Buy or change a ticket
Handle destination/time/fare/detail exchange and one less-predictable change or clarification.

### U7-L04 — Make, change or cancel a reservation
Reach the practical outcome across spoken/written/phone channels; channel mechanics are support, not the primary capability.

### U7-L05 — Handle accommodation or journey problems
Explain what is wrong, understand a proposed resolution and negotiate a straightforward alternative.

### U7-L06 — Manage a less-routine travel conversation with repair
Integrate transport/accommodation information, question reformulation, checking and circumlocution when an expected detail changes.

### U7-L07 — Fresh travel mission
New itinerary with a disruption: extract information → navigate/choose → change a booking/ticket → resolve one misunderstanding. **Zero planned new required content.**

**Unit critic:** detailed directions, transaction changes and travel problem resolution share context but retain distinct outcomes; no generic “travel vocabulary” lesson.

# Unit 8 — Shopping, Services, Complaints, and Equipment Problems
**7 slots**  
Primary capability: **C13**; cross-links C07/C08.  
Lexical homes: L08, L09, L21/L22.  
Exit path: explicit **unscripted familiar problem** evidence.

Purpose: extend routine A2 service transactions into B1 needs/problems, product comparison, delivery/payment arrangements, troubleshooting and complaints.

### U8-L01 — Explain what you need and ask for suitable options
Describe requirements/constraints sufficiently for another person to suggest a product/service, rather than naming a memorised item only.

### U8-L02 — Compare price, value, quantity and suitability
Use practical comparison/trend/degree language to evaluate alternatives and make a reasoned service/product choice.

### U8-L03 — Arrange payment, delivery or another service detail
Clarify dates/conditions/details, record key information accurately and confirm the arrangement.

### U8-L04 — Explain a product or equipment problem
Describe symptoms/behaviour of a familiar device/product, when the problem occurs and what has already been tried.

### U8-L05 — Understand and follow troubleshooting/resolution advice
Interpret straightforward solution steps, ask for clarification and confirm what to do next; passive/instruction language may support reception.

### U8-L06 — Make a complaint and request action
State the problem, relevant evidence and requested remedy politely but clearly in spoken/written service interaction.

### U8-L07 — Unscripted familiar-problem evidence
Unseen familiar service/equipment problem with incomplete/changing information: diagnose communicatively (not technically), clarify, compare solutions and reach an outcome. **Zero planned new required content.**

# Unit 12 — B1 Integration and Exit Evidence
**5 slots — all zero-new**  
Purpose: fresh cumulative evidence under changed prompts with reduced support. These slots introduce **no planned new required source system or lexical content**.

### U12-L01 — Unprepared familiar conversation + repair
Enter and sustain a new familiar-topic conversation, manage turns, respond to an unexpected detail and use clarification/circumlocution successfully.

### U12-L02 — Reasoned response transfer — Context 2
Different domain/task from U3-L08: interpret brief input, state viewpoint, compare options, give reasons and respond to another position. Satisfies the required “more than one context” spacing when reconciled with U3-L08.

### U12-L03 — Fresh connected narrative
New event/prompt with no complete model: organise background, sequence, prior information and outcome/reaction into connected spoken or written narrative evidence.

### U12-L04 — Source-to-response mediation
New clear sources: select/summarise/compare useful information and relay it to a specified person/purpose, then answer a clarification or produce an appropriate connected response.

### U12-L05 — Integrated familiar problem mission
Combine practical information + service/travel/problem interaction + repair + short written follow-up in a changed scenario. Evidence should show B1 independent familiar-situation operation, not memory of a practised script.

**Unit critic:** final integration is evidence, not a hiding place for leftover inventory. Stage 06 must mechanically enforce zero new introductions in all five slots.

# Whole-map dependency critic

## 1. Independence grows early, not only at exit
U1 immediately shifts from A2 routine exchange toward unprepared conversation/repair. Later units repeatedly reduce support.

## 2. Reasoned response is spaced
U3 develops it and U3-L08 gives Context 1; U12-L02 deliberately uses a different context for Context 2. C07 resources also recur in travel/services/media/presentation.

## 3. Narrative has enough runway
U4 introduces/reconciles reception, sequence, background, prior time, current relevance and change before fresh narrative evidence. U12-L03 provides later retrieval.

## 4. Mediation/reporting is not postponed
U5 prepares source comprehension; U6 explicitly transforms sources; U10 notes/presentation and U11 response chains reuse selection/reporting; U12-L04 retrieves it later.

## 5. Grammar prerequisites precede dependent performance
A2 systems are retrieved before B1 expansions. Comparison/reason resources precede later product/travel choices; narrative past systems precede later reporting; source comprehension precedes mediation; discourse/reference systems recur before formal connected writing/presentation.

## 6. Broad lexical topics do not control the syllabus
Environment, public life, science, culture, business and sport appear as contexts across U3–U6/U10/U11 rather than becoming content-only units.

## 7. Writing is distributed by genre
Narrative writing lives in U4, practical writing in U9, presentation notes in U10, correspondence/online interaction in U11, and reasoned/source-based writing can appear in U3/U6/U12. No single final “writing unit” carries the whole obligation.

## 8. Pronunciation is distributed
Connected-speech perception starts U1/U5; prominence/chunking becomes explicit in U10; grammatical endings/stress attach to narrative/explanation; intonation/repair recur in interaction. No isolated pronunciation chapter.

## 9. Transfer slots are protected
All 11 unit-end transfers and all 5 U12 integration slots are explicitly **zero planned new content**. Stage 06 exact ledger must fail if required new records leak into them.

## 10. Count honesty
The **12-unit / 84-slot** shape is a consequence of the current human performance/dependency map. It is not protected for product symmetry. Stage 06 may split/merge/move slots when exact source-record load or lifecycle evidence provides a concrete reason.
