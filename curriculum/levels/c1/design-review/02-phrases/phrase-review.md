- `X-REGISTER-AUDIENCE` — formality, neutrality, conventions, audience/register fit

Stage 2 adds one **cross-cutting refinement candidate**, not a new capability:

- `X-EVIDENTIAL-ATTRIBUTION` — report/attribute findings, claims, agency or evidence with an appropriate source/evidential frame. This is motivated by repeated C1 phrase evidence such as `be attributed to`, `be found to`, `be reported to`, `be said to`, `in the light of`, and `on the part of`. It supports C01/C06/C08/C09/C10/C11 and must later be reconciled with Stage-3 reporting/passive grammar.

`G3` in a boundary note means the phrase has a grammar interface to reconcile in Stage 3; the phrase itself does not license the full grammar system.

## Architecture feedback from Stage 2

### 1. No new top-level capability family

The 75 phrases strengthen the existing C01–C12 architecture. None supplies an independent learner-performance boundary that is missing strongly enough to justify C13.

### 2. Add `X-EVIDENTIAL-ATTRIBUTION` as a cross-cutting refinement

Repeated phrase evidence distinguishes C1 from a generic advanced-language list: learners need to handle attribution, findings, reported claims, agency and evidence-conditioned conclusions without turning each reporting phrase into an isolated grammar target.

This refinement must later connect to:

- Stage 3 reporting/passive/evidential grammar;
- C01/C03 reception of source stance/reporting;
- C06/C10 evidence-based argument;
- C08/C09 source transformation;
- C11 formal/professional reporting.

It remains a **cross-cutting requirement**, not a capability or unit.

### 3. Formal relation/topic framing is a strong C1 phrase pattern

`as to`, `in accordance with`, `in connection with`, `in respect of`, `with regard to`, `with respect to`, `on behalf of`, and related frames strengthen `X-REGISTER-AUDIENCE` and C11/C09/C10. They do not justify a standalone “formal phrases” unit.

### 4. Qualification, contrast and rhetorical scaling recur

`above all`, `all but`, `all the more`, `as opposed to`, `by no means`, `for that matter`, `in any case`, `let alone`, `little more than`, `not to mention`, `nothing like`, and `with the exception of` reinforce C1 discourse/rhetorical control. Stage 3 must check where grammar interfaces exist, but phrase evidence should not be inflated into independent grammar systems.

### 5. Polysemy and phrasal-verb safeguards

Items such as `bring sth up`, `dispose of`, `get away with`, `live on`, `put it`, and `up against` are source-sense bounded. Stage 4 word review must not make the base word inherit all phrase senses, and later lesson authoring must not silently generalise these phrases to unrelated phrasal meanings.

## Stage-3 handoff flags

Grammar review should explicitly inspect interfaces around:

- reporting/passive/evidential frames: `be attributed to`, `be found to`, `be reported to`, `be said to`, `set to do`;
- stance/probability: `may well`;
- premise/condition/result: `given that`, `in the event of`, `in the absence of`, `in such a way (that)`;
- comparison/degree/emphasis: `all but`, `all the more`, `by no means`, `little more than`, `the extent to which`;
- complementation/formal intention: `be meant to`, `in a position to`, `seek to do`;
- formal prepositional/discourse frames: `as of`, `as to`, `in accordance with`, `in conjunction with`, `in respect of`, `with regard to`, `with respect to`.

Phrase evidence is a boundary/input for Stage 3, not permission to declare those grammar systems mastered.
