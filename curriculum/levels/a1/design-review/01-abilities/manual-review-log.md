## Decision vocabulary

- **KEEP** — the source ability expresses a meaningful learner performance that must remain visible in the final capability map.
- **MERGE** — preserve the source evidence, but represent it as a component of a broader final capability.
- **SUPPORTING** — a general processing/language condition rather than a useful standalone curriculum capability.
- **SPLIT** — the source statement contains more than one materially distinct learner performance.

## Provisional capability families after Batch 2

The following are provisional only; later abilities can merge, split, rename, or broaden them.

1. `CAP-TIME-DATES-NUMBERS` — understand and communicate basic functional numbers, times, dates and number strings such as phone numbers; price use also links to transactions.
2. `CAP-UNDERSTAND-DESCRIPTIONS` — understand/identify simple descriptions of familiar people, places, objects and animals across reading/listening.
3. `CAP-DESCRIBE-OBJECTS` — describe familiar objects using basic properties such as colour and size.
4. `CAP-UNDERSTAND-LOCATION` / `CAP-DESCRIBE-LOCATION` — currently separated by reception vs production; may later become one cross-modal location capability with visible sub-boundaries.
5. `CAP-FOLLOW-WRITTEN-INSTRUCTIONS` — follow short written routes/procedures.
6. `CAP-FOLLOW-SPOKEN-INSTRUCTIONS` — follow simple spoken commands/directions/routes under A1 delivery conditions.
7. `CAP-UNDERSTAND-SIGNS-NOTICES` — understand short public signs/notices.
8. `CAP-LOCATE-PERSONAL-DETAILS` — locate requested personal details in simple written sources.
9. `CAP-LOCATE-SPOKEN-KEY-INFO` — pick out names/places and similar key information from short speech/dialogue.
10. `CAP-UNDERSTAND-SIMPLE-ARRANGEMENTS` — understand basic time/place information for meetings/events.
11. `CAP-UNDERSTAND-INTRODUCTIONS` — understand the main personal information in simple introductions.
12. `CAP-INTRODUCE-SELF` — give a basic self-introduction with key personal information.
13. `CAP-UNDERSTAND-PREFERENCES` — understand likes/dislikes and questions about them.
14. `CAP-UNDERSTAND-ROUTINES-LEISURE` — understand basic information/questions about familiar activities/free time.
15. `CAP-UNDERSTAND-ABILITIES` — understand what someone can/cannot do.
16. `CAP-FOLLOW-BASIC-TRANSACTIONS` — follow the meaning of slow, clear everyday transactions.
17. `CAP-HANDLE-BASIC-TRANSACTIONS` — umbrella candidate for productive/interactive transaction components; may split by domain later.
18. `CAP-ORDER-FOOD-DRINK` — request food/drink in a basic service encounter.
19. `CAP-BUY-TRANSPORT-TICKETS` — complete a basic public-transport ticket purchase.
20. `CAP-EXCHANGE-FAMILY-FRIEND-INFO` — ask and answer simple questions about family/friends.
21. `CAP-ASK-FOR-SPELLING-CLARIFICATION` — request spelling/written form as communication repair.
22. `CAP-RESPOND-TO-REQUESTS-OFFERS` — give a basic acceptance/response to requests/offers.
23. `CAP-BASIC-SOCIAL-CONTACT` — greet, ask about wellbeing, and make simple social reactions.
24. `CAP-UNDERSTAND-SIMPLE-QUESTIONS` — temporary umbrella for understanding basic questions; likely to dissolve into semantic capabilities where question meaning is domain-specific.

## Critic flags currently open

- `CAP-TIME-DATES-NUMBERS` is becoming broad. Later objectives must show whether numbers, time/date, and price should be one capability family with sub-capabilities or separate final capabilities.
- Written vs spoken instruction-following may be separate capabilities or modalities inside one larger `FOLLOW-SIMPLE-INSTRUCTIONS` family. Do not collapse until production/interaction evidence is reviewed.
- `CAP-HANDLE-BASIC-TRANSACTIONS` is only an umbrella candidate. Food ordering and transport tickets already look distinct enough to stay visible even if grouped in one unit later.
- Generic processing records (`understand basic facts`, `recognise familiar phrases`, `very slow speech`) are being marked SUPPORTING, not discarded. They must reappear later as task/design/assessment conditions.
- Description coverage spans people, places, objects, animals and spatial position. Do not treat one successful description task as proof of all domains.
