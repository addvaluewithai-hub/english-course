## Decision vocabulary

- **KEEP** — meaningful learner performance that should remain visible in the final capability map.
- **MERGE** — preserve the source evidence but represent it inside a broader capability.
- **SUPPORTING** — processing condition, mechanics, or enabling skill rather than a useful standalone capability.
- **SPLIT** — source statement contains materially different performances that belong in more than one capability.

## Consolidation observations before Final Capability Map

1. **Numbers/time/dates/quantity** are clearly cross-modal and repeated often enough to justify one capability family with explicit sub-capabilities rather than separate capabilities for every modality/precision point.
2. **Descriptions** need separation by semantic purpose: object information, location, weather, and broad receptive description are not all the same task even though they share adjective/preposition language.
3. **Personal information** has several related but non-identical performances: understanding introductions, introducing self, introducing others, exchanging personal details, family/friend information, home information, and possessions.
4. **Transactions** should be a family, not one giant capability. Food/drink ordering, transport tickets, hotel check-in, prices, and following a transaction share language but have different success conditions.
5. **Communication management** (attention, spelling/clarification) is a real learner-survival capability; repetition/read-aloud mechanics are supporting evidence, not endpoints.
6. **Writing mechanics** (capitalisation, punctuation, copying, spelling) should become quality/constraint requirements attached to writing capabilities rather than four extra lessons/capabilities.
7. Generic source descriptors about understanding “basic/familiar language” or speech being “slow and clear” are level conditions/support, not learner-purpose capabilities.
