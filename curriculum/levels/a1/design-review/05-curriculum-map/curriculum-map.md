# A1 Curriculum Map — Manual Lesson Skeleton

Status: **LOCKED FOR DETAILED COVERAGE MAPPING**  
Stage: **05 — Curriculum / Lesson Mapping**

Inputs reviewed manually before this map:
- 119/119 required abilities;
- 199/199 Oxford A1 phrases;
- 109/109 canonical A1 EGP grammar features;
- 900/900 Oxford A1 word records;
- 10 pronunciation progression strands;
- A1 exit contract and assessment principles.

No old 45-lesson sequence, automated cluster map, or target lesson count was used to generate this structure.

# Design rules

1. **Performance first.** Each lesson has one primary learner performance; language content serves it.
2. **Capabilities are not lessons.** A capability may span several lessons; a lesson may recycle several capabilities.
3. **No vocabulary dumps.** Lexical sets appear inside usable contexts/tasks.
4. **No grammar chapter sequence.** Grammar enters when a communicative performance needs it.
5. **Phrase/chunk priority.** Natural chunks are introduced with their task and later analysed/reused where useful.
6. **Pronunciation is embedded.** The 10 pronunciation strands are distributed across language learners actually use.
7. **Coverage is not mastery.** Every required item later needs introduce → practise → fresh retrieval → recycle → consolidate → exit verify as appropriate.
8. **Fresh retrieval changes the prompt/context.** Unit-end work may not simply repeat the lesson model.
9. **No complete-model copying at mastery checks.** Support may clarify, but may not supply the response.
10. **Lesson count emerges from load.** No lesson exists merely to preserve a chosen product number.

# Locked skeleton shape

- **10 units**
- **62 lesson slots**
- **57 instructional/retrieval slots + 5 final integration/exit slots**
- unit sizes vary from 5 to 7 lessons because the content load varies.

The critic tested the proposed 62 slots and did **not** find padding sufficient to justify cutting them. With 900 lexical records, 199 phrases, 109 grammar features, 23 capability families, pronunciation, writing, retrieval and transfer, a materially smaller skeleton would push too much new content into consecutive lessons. Conversely, 62 is not a permanent commercial promise: the detailed item-to-lesson ledger may split or merge a slot only when concrete overload/underload evidence requires it.

The critic **did** change sequencing: the original candidate left past/future language too close to exit and used Unit 7 as a broad catch-all. The locked order introduces productive past in Unit 7, plans/future in Unit 8, and uses Unit 9 as a coherent practical-text/retrieval unit so both time systems receive later fresh use before exit.

# Writing thread — critic resolution

Writing is not deferred to one late unit. Required writing is distributed as follows:
- U1-L05/L07 — spelling/contact details/basic form;
- U2/U3 transfer tasks — short person/home/object information where appropriate;
- U4 — times/schedules/routine information;
- U6-L06 — travel/personal-detail form;
- U7-L04/L05 — short past factual information;
- U9-L05/L06 — explicit personal message/practical writing;
- U10 — fresh integrated writing evidence.

This is sufficient for A1's isolated-phrase/simple-sentence writing boundary without turning every lesson into a writing lesson.

# C14 signs/notices — critic resolution

C14 receives:
- first explicit evidence in **U6-L01**;
- reuse inside **U6-L07**;
- a second explicit, differently contextualised reading task in **U9-L01**;
- integration in **U9-L06** and **U10-L02**.

Therefore it is not underrepresented despite having no dedicated Oxford phrase cluster.

# C17 repair — critic resolution

C17 is introduced early in **U1-L05** because learners need it to survive the course itself. It then recurs as a behaviour in partner tasks, requests/directions/services, pronunciation monitoring, and the exit bundle. A separate late “repair lesson” would be less useful than continuous required use.

# Pronunciation distribution

The pronunciation strands are not separate chapters:

- **Sound perception / word recognition:** U1 onward; names, numbers, prices, directions and listening tasks.
- **Consonant clarity:** U1–U3 names/jobs/family; recycled across key nouns/verbs.
- **Vowel clarity:** selected high-value contrasts in personal, food, place and everyday vocabulary; no exhaustive phoneme syllabus.
- **Syllable shape / clusters / endings:** U2–U9, especially plurals, jobs, place words, commands and transport.
- **Grammatical endings:** plurals/possessives early; selected verb/past endings where meaning depends on them.
- **Word stress:** names, numbers, jobs, place/transport vocabulary across U1–U6 and later retrieval.
- **Prominence/rhythm:** exposure through learnt chunks, especially service/social/plan language.
- **Connected speech:** receptive exposure in frequent social/service chunks, not natural-speed mastery.
- **Intonation/stance:** greetings/questions U1, requests U5, suggestions/invitations U8.
- **Chunking/monitoring/repair:** explicit U1-L05, recycled throughout; exit tasks include repair opportunity.

# Lifecycle requirement for detailed mapping

For every required source item, the next ledger must identify as appropriate:

1. **Introduction/home** — first meaningful presentation/use.
2. **Immediate practice** — controlled/guided use while fresh.
3. **Fresh retrieval** — later changed prompt/context.
4. **Cross-unit recycle** — enough spacing to require retrieval rather than imitation.
5. **Consolidation** — integrated unit/spiral task.
6. **Exit verification** — capability/item evidence appropriate to its role; no required content may disappear by accident.

# Manual critic result

The critic challenged the candidate against the following risks:

1. **62 lessons/padding:** retained. Transfer slots have a different job from instruction and are necessary for fresh retrieval; they are not decorative review lessons.
2. **Unit 7 catch-all:** fixed. Past language now owns U7; practical literacy becomes coherent U9.
3. **C08 duplication:** accepted as distributed retrieval. U2 focuses people, U3 objects/home, U9 mixed receptive descriptions/factual profiles.
4. **Writing underweight:** fixed/documented through the writing thread above.
5. **C14 signs/notices:** sufficient spaced evidence now explicit.
6. **C17 repair:** early explicit teaching plus continuous behavioural requirement is preferable to a late standalone lesson.
7. **900-word load:** 62 is a defensible baseline because lexical/function words are partly integrated through phrases/grammar and multiple slots are retrieval-only. Exact overload must still be tested by the detailed source-record ledger.
8. **Past too late:** fixed by moving productive past to U7, followed by U9/U10 retrieval.
9. **Future too late:** C13 is U8 but has earlier lexical/chunk exposure in time/invitation/travel contexts and explicit U9/U10 fresh retrieval.
10. **Exit repetition:** U10 uses five different evidence bundles rather than repeating one unit-end task.
