# A1 Curriculum Map — Manual Lesson Skeleton

Status: **CANDIDATE SKELETON — MANUAL CRITIC REQUIRED BEFORE LOCK**  
Stage: **05 — Curriculum / Lesson Mapping**

Inputs already reviewed manually:
- 119/119 required abilities;
- 199/199 Oxford A1 phrases;
- 109/109 canonical A1 EGP grammar features;
- 900/900 Oxford A1 word records;
- 10 pronunciation progression strands;
- A1 exit contract and assessment principles.

No old 45-lesson sequence, automated cluster map, or target lesson count was used to generate this structure.

# Design rules

1. **Performance first.** Each lesson has one primary learner performance; language content serves it.
2. **Capabilities are not lessons.** A capability may span several lessons; a lesson may recycle several capabilities.
3. **No vocabulary dumps.** Lexical sets appear inside usable contexts/tasks.
4. **No grammar chapter sequence.** Grammar enters when a communicative performance needs it.
5. **Phrase/chunk priority.** Natural chunks are introduced with their task and later analysed/reused where useful.
6. **Pronunciation is embedded.** The 10 pronunciation strands are distributed across language that learners actually use.
7. **Coverage is not mastery.** Every required item later needs a lifecycle: introduce → practise → fresh retrieval → recycle → consolidate → exit verify.
8. **Fresh retrieval matters.** Unit-end tasks must change the prompt/context enough that learners cannot merely repeat the lesson model.
9. **No complete-model copying at mastery checks.** Support may clarify, but may not supply the response.
10. **Lesson count emerges from load.** The count below is the result of the manual content architecture, not a preset product number.

# Proposed shape

- **10 units**
- **62 lessons total**
- **57 instructional/retrieval lessons + 5 final integration/exit lessons**
- Unit sizes vary from 5 to 7 lessons because the content load varies.

Why 62 is plausible:
- 900 lexical records average roughly 14–15 records per lesson before accounting for function words/grammar-owned items and repeated/recycled vocabulary;
- 199 phrases average about 3 per lesson but are unevenly concentrated in routines/time/social language;
- 109 canonical grammar features are mostly structural manifestations, not 109 separate teaching points;
- 23 capabilities need repeated evidence across modalities and contexts;
- several lessons deliberately carry little/no new language so they can provide retrieval and transfer.

The number is still challengeable by the critic below; no lesson should survive merely to preserve “62.”

# Pronunciation distribution

The pronunciation strands are not separate chapters:

- **Sound perception / word recognition:** starts U1; recycled in names, numbers, prices, directions and listening tasks.
- **Consonant clarity:** U1–U3 names/jobs/family; recycled across key nouns/verbs.
- **Vowel clarity:** selected high-value contrasts in personal, food, place and everyday vocabulary; model/recycle rather than exhaustive phoneme syllabus.
- **Syllable shape / clusters / endings:** U2–U7, especially plurals, jobs, place words, commands and transport.
- **Grammatical endings:** plurals/possessives early; selected verb/past endings where meaning depends on them.
- **Word stress:** names, numbers, jobs, place/transport vocabulary across U1–U6.
- **Prominence/rhythm:** exposed through learnt chunks, especially service/social/plan language.
- **Connected speech:** receptive exposure in frequent social/service chunks, not natural-speed mastery.
- **Intonation/stance:** greetings/questions U1, requests U5, suggestions/invitations U8.
- **Chunking/monitoring/repair:** explicit in U1-L05 and continually recycled; exit task includes repair opportunity.

# Lifecycle rule for later detailed mapping

For every required source item, the lesson ledger must eventually identify:

1. **Introduction/home** — first meaningful presentation/use.
2. **Immediate practice** — controlled/guided use while the form/meaning is fresh.
3. **Fresh retrieval** — a later lesson with a changed prompt/context.
4. **Cross-unit recycle** — use after enough spacing to require retrieval rather than short-term imitation.
5. **Consolidation** — integrated unit/spiral task.
6. **Exit verification** — where appropriate to the item/capability; not every lexical record needs an isolated test item, but no required content can disappear from the exit contract by accident.

# Manual critic questions

1. **62 lessons:** Is the count genuinely driven by load, or have some transfer lessons become formulaic padding?
2. **Unit 7:** Is it coherent enough, or is it a catch-all for instructions/descriptions/writing?
3. **C08:** Is receptive description sufficiently integrated throughout Units 2/3/7, or does U7 duplicate earlier work?
4. **Writing:** Are C20 forms and C21 personal messages distributed enough, or is writing underweighted compared with speaking?
5. **C14 signs/notices:** Is one main lesson plus travel reuse sufficient for the required reading/mediation evidence?
6. **C17 repair:** Is early explicit teaching + continuous recycling enough despite no dedicated later lesson?
7. **900 words:** Do 62 lessons provide realistic room for meaningful introduction plus recycling without creating 15–20 new lexical items in too many consecutive lessons?
8. **Past placement:** Is Unit 9 too late for past simple to receive adequate later recycling before exit?
9. **Future placement:** Same question for C13 in Unit 8.
10. **Exit unit:** Does Unit 10 give fresh evidence rather than simply repeat unit-end tasks?

No detailed lesson authoring should begin until this skeleton passes the critic and the unit/lesson distribution is adjusted in this same file.
