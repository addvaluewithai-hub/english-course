## Architecture conclusions

1. **Add a new capability for simple past events/states.** EGP explicitly gives A1 productive past-simple form and use for everyday events/states. This is a real communicative performance, not just grammar infrastructure.
2. **Expand C06** so it includes both habitual/routine activity and simple activity/event-in-progress language. EGP present continuous plus GSE present-simple-vs-present-continuous evidence makes this a coherent contrast inside one everyday-activity capability.
3. **C13 is confirmed.** EGP has A1 `will/'ll` for plans and intentions. Oxford A1 also lists `be going to do sth`, but the reviewed grammar-family progression places full `be going to` productive control at A2; therefore at A1 it should be treated cautiously as phrase/chunk evidence rather than proof of full productive grammar-system control.
4. **C22 is confirmed.** GSE Grammar explicitly supports present simple for opinions/likes/dislikes; EGP `but`, adjective/evaluation structures and reason clauses reinforce the language needed for simple evaluation/opinion.
5. **Expand C18 to include simple invitations.** EGP A1 includes `would like to invite`; this belongs with requests/offers rather than a new capability.
6. **Comparison stays cross-cutting, not top-level.** There is no canonical A1 EGP comparative feature; reviewed comparative families are planned exposure with first productive control at A2. The Oxford phrase `differences between A and B` therefore does not justify an A1 comparison capability.
7. **Add a cross-cutting core grammar infrastructure requirement.** Articles, noun phrases, basic clause formation, pronouns, negation, plural marking, linking `be`, and similar structural features are mandatory across many capabilities but should not generate fake communicative capabilities.
8. **Add simple possibility with `can` as cross-cutting semantic control.** One A1 EGP feature uses `can` for possibility; this is required but too small to justify its own top-level capability.

# Grammar-driven architecture changes to apply

## A. C06 — expand current-activity scope
Change from `Talk about routines and free-time activities` to:

**Talk about routines, free-time activities, and what is happening now.**

Mandatory branches:
- repeated routines/habits;
- free-time activities;
- frequency;
- a simple current activity/event in progress;
- distinguish habitual vs happening-now meaning in simple familiar contexts.

## B. C13 — future/plans confirmed
Keep `Understand arrangements and talk about simple plans`, with an explicit grammar note:
- productive A1 grammar evidence includes `will/'ll` for plans and intentions;
- `be going to do sth` is an Oxford A1 phrase, but full `be going to` grammar-family productive control is planned at A2 in the reviewed progression. At A1, teach/use it cautiously as the source-evidenced phrase/chunk rather than assuming broad system mastery.

## C. C18 — add invitation branch
Expand requests/offers to include a **simple invitation** branch because EGP A1 includes `would like to invite`.

## D. Add C23 — Talk about simple past events and states
Learner can give very simple information about a familiar past event or state using A1 past-simple language.

Canonical evidence:
- EGP row 887 — past simple affirmative form with a limited range of regular/irregular verbs;
- EGP row 889 — past simple used for everyday events or states.

Natural phrase support already present in Stage 02 includes `last year`, `in the past`, `X years ago`, `X years later`, `get married`, `grow up`, and `the next day`.

Do not infer extended narration, detailed storytelling, or full past-system mastery at A1.

## E. Add X10 — Core grammar infrastructure
Mandatory across capabilities, but not a communicative capability by itself:
- simple affirmative/negative clause building;
- linking/auxiliary `be`;
- basic articles and noun phrases;
- common plural marking;
- subject/object pronouns and basic reference;
- core noun-phrase grammatical functions;
- general present-simple form where required by the communicative home;
- basic negation.

## F. Add X11 — Simple possibility with `can`
A1 EGP requires `can` for possibility. Keep this as a cross-cutting semantic control unless later word/task evidence shows a substantial independent performance. It may naturally appear in weather, places, activities, plans, and simple factual descriptions.

## G. X08 — strengthen reason/contrast/expansion
Canonical grammar confirms `because`, `and`, `but`, and `or` as A1 resources for joining and extending simple language. X08 therefore owns simple reason, contrast, coordination, examples, and basic answer expansion across capabilities.

## H. X09 — comparison decision confirmed
No A1 canonical comparative feature appears in the 109-item EGP inventory. Reviewed comparative families are planned exposure with first productive control at A2. Keep basic comparison language cross-cutting at A1; do not create a top-level comparison capability.
