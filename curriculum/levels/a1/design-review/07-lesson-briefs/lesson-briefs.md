## Instructional-load rule

A source record being introduced in a lesson does **not** mean every record becomes a separate explicit teaching objective.

Each brief distinguishes:
- **Foreground language/performance** — actively noticed, practised, and needed for the task.
- **Embedded introduction/support** — encountered meaningfully in models, texts, images, examples, instructions, or contrast sets without being turned into an isolated drill.
- **Recycle** — previously encountered language used again under a changed prompt/context.
- **Evidence task** — observable learner performance that determines whether the lesson objective was achieved.

Several EGP features or source abilities can describe one learner performance. Likewise, a lexical set can contain many words that belong naturally in one context. The exact ledger proves that no source record disappears and that every required record receives later retrieval and verification.

## Authoring guardrails

1. One clear primary learner performance per instructional lesson.
2. Do not turn every source record into a slide, definition, or isolated drill.
3. Chunks come before unnecessary grammar explanation.
4. Grammar explanation is bounded to what the learner needs for the task.
5. Broad lexical sets use a manageable active core plus supported recognition and later retrieval.
6. Pronunciation is attached to language the learner is actually using.
7. Unit-end transfer lessons introduce no hidden new system; they change the context and require retrieval.
8. A complete model may support learning earlier in a lesson, but mastery evidence may not be copied from a complete model.
9. Support may clarify the task or individual language, but may not supply the learner's final response.
10. If real authoring shows that a brief cannot fit without rushing or superficial exposure, split the lesson and update the 62-slot architecture rather than compressing it artificially.

# Unit 1 — First Contact: Me and You

Unit purpose: give a new A1 learner enough language to survive a first social encounter, exchange core identity/contact information, handle basic numbers and spelling repair, and complete/use a simple personal profile.

Primary capabilities: **C01, C02, C11, C17, C20**.  
Main cross-cutting requirements: **X03, X04, X05, X06, X07, X10**.  
Unit-end transfer: **U1-L07**.

## U1-L01 — Hello. I'm …

### Primary performance
Open a very simple first-contact exchange, exchange names, ask/answer basic wellbeing, and respond politely.

### Foreground language
- `Hello / Hi.`
- `Good morning.`
- `I'm … / My name is …`
- `What's your name?`
- `How are you?`
- `I'm fine / pretty good / really good.`
- `Thank you / Thanks.`

### Grammar boundary
Use only the minimum first-/second-person `be` and simple question/answer frames needed for the encounter. Multiple EGP rows in this slot are not separate grammar chapters.

### Lexical handling
Foreground the words that make the encounter work: name, hello/hi, morning, good/fine, thank/thanks, person/people where useful. Other assigned words may enter through supported examples rather than isolated recall.

### Pronunciation
Names, short learnt chunks, basic greeting/question contour, and intelligibility rather than accent imitation.

### Evidence task
With a new identity/name prompt and no complete script, the learner greets, exchanges names, handles `How are you?`, and gives one polite response.

### Planned retrieval
U1-L03 new-partner greeting; U1-L06 wider social response; U1-L07 fresh integrated exchange.

### Do not do
Do not teach the news/reaction cluster here; human critic moved it to U1-L06. Do not explain the whole `be` system or accept a memorised full dialogue as mastery evidence.

### Ledger load
**8 abilities / 5 phrases / 8 grammar records / 13 word records / 2 pronunciation records = 36 source introductions.**

## U1-L02 — How old are you?

### Primary performance
Give and understand age and basic functional numbers in a personal-information exchange.

### Foreground language
- required basic number patterns;
- `How old are you?`
- `I'm … years old.` / `X years old`.

### Human-critic decision
`millions of`, `thousands of`, and `more than…` were moved to U5-L04 quantities. They are valid A1 inventory items but do not belong in an age lesson.

### Lexical/grammar handling
Teach the number system as patterns and meaningful number use, not as unrelated flashcards. Keep grammar limited to the age question/answer frame.

### Pronunciation
Number contrast recognition and intelligible production of easily confused number forms.

### Evidence task
Fresh age/number prompts requiring understanding and production without copying the lesson model.

### Planned retrieval
U1-L05 phone/contact strings; U1-L07 profile transfer; Unit 5 quantities/prices; Unit 6 ticket information.

### Ledger load
**4 abilities / 1 phrase / 2 grammar records / 21 word records / 1 pronunciation record = 29 source introductions.**

## U1-L04 — What do you do?

### Primary performance
Give and understand very basic job/study information as part of a personal profile.

### Foreground language
- `I'm a …`
- `I work as …`
- `I work for …` where transparent and natural;
- student/work/job language.

Oxford phrases: `work as sth`, `work for sb/sth`.

### Lexical handling
Twenty-three word records are assigned here. Use three layers:
1. active core of high-value role words plus `job/work/student`;
2. supported recognition/sorting for the wider occupation set;
3. later retrieval in Unit 2, Unit 4 and Unit 7.

The learner should leave able to exchange a job/study fact, not merely recite an occupation list.

### Evidence task
Fresh profile/person image: state a basic job/student fact and understand the same kind of fact from another person.

### Ledger load
**5 abilities / 2 phrases / 0 new grammar records / 23 word records = 30 source introductions.**

### Primary performance

### Foreground language

Oxford phrase cluster:

- `phone number`

Required authored repair language because the ability source requires the function:
- `How do you spell that?`

### Writing/pronunciation
Letters/digits, common names, basic capitalisation, accurate-enough transcription, and intelligible letter/name/number production.

### Evidence task
Fresh information gap: exchange contact details and use repair when a key item is unclear.

### Planned retrieval
U1-L07 profile task; U6-L06 travel/hotel form; ongoing repair across later speaking tasks and exit evidence.

### Ledger load
**7 abilities / 5 phrases / 1 grammar record / 22 word records / 1 pronunciation record = 36 source introductions.**

## U1-L06 — This is my friend …

### Primary performance
Introduce another person, respond socially to an introduction/simple piece of news, and close the encounter.

### Foreground interaction routines
1. introduce/respond;
2. react to simple positive/negative news;
3. welcome/polite response;
4. leave-taking.

Language includes:
- `This is …`
- basic `he / she / him` reference;
- `someone else`
- `speak to sb`
- `See you`
- `good news / bad news`
- `Guess what…`
- `Oh yes / Oh no!`
- `I'm sorry`
- `welcome to sth`
- `You're welcome`

### Recycle
Names/wellbeing from L01, origin from L03, job/study from L04.

### Grammar boundary
Basic third-person reference and short `be` statements only; no full pronoun-system lecture.

### Evidence task
Fresh person card + social cue: introduce the person, give one or two known facts, react appropriately, and close the exchange without a complete script.

### Load warning
The 12 phrase records are accepted only because they form four coherent interaction routines. If real authoring turns them into twelve independent chunk drills, split the slot.

### Ledger load
**4 abilities / 12 phrases / 2 grammar records / 22 word records = 40 source introductions.**

## U1-L07 — Who is this person? — Unit 1 Transfer

### Primary performance
Complete and use a **new** personal profile by integrating Unit-1 language rather than repeating one lesson model.

### Transfer bundle
Require at least two modalities. A strong sequence is:
1. read/listen for missing personal details;
2. ask for remaining information;
3. complete a simple profile/form;
4. use the information in a short unscripted exchange/introduction.

### Success criteria
The learner retrieves rather than copies, initiates at least one relevant question, understands/responds to basic questions, records information accurately enough for the purpose, and repairs a communication problem if the task creates one.

### New-content boundary
No hidden new grammar/vocabulary system. The phrase `find out` may appear naturally in task framing but may not turn the transfer lesson into a new phrase chapter.

### Ledger load
**1 ability / 1 phrase / 0 grammar / 0 new word / 0 pronunciation = 2 source introductions.**

# Unit 2 — People Around Me

Unit purpose: move from self-information to family/familiar people, descriptions, preferences/interests, and ability/inability while preserving clear reception/production boundaries.

Primary capabilities: **C03, C05, C07, C08**, with **X01** ownership/possession support and continued C02/X03/X04/X10 recycling.  
Main lexical homes: **L02, L03, L06, L19, L25**.  
Pronunciation focus: family/job contrasts, plural/possessive endings, word stress, and `can/can't` intelligibility where useful.  
Unit-end transfer: **U2-L06**.

## U2-L01 — Who's in your family?

### Primary performance
Understand and give very basic information about family relationships and familiar people.

### Foreground language
Family/relationship core:
- mother/father/parent, brother/sister, son/daughter, child, husband/wife, friend/partner and other high-value family terms;
- `This is my …`
- `I've got / have got …` where natural;
- `his / her / their` for simple reference;
- simple third-person facts already supported by Unit 1.

Oxford phrases introduced here:
- `best friend`
- `each other`
- `family member`
- `live with sb`
- `have got`
- `his or her`

### Human-critic decision
`get married` and `grow up` are **not** introduced here; they are already placed in U7-L03 with life-event/past language. Do not pull them forward merely because they are semantically related to family.

### Grammar foreground
- possessive determiners;
- bounded `have got` family use;
- regular plural marking where needed;
- the lexicalised phrase `my best friend` without treating it as a comparative/superlative grammar chapter.

### Lexical handling
The family set is broad. Foreground the relationships needed for a usable small family/friend network. Less central kinship words can enter through supported family trees and recognition tasks, then return in U2-L03/U2-L06 and later factual profiles.

### Pronunciation
- clear contrasts in key family words;
- plural endings where meaningful;
- possessive/reference forms intelligibly enough for relationship identification.

### Evidence task
Given a new family/friend diagram, the learner identifies relationships and gives two or three simple facts about familiar people without copying a complete model.

### Planned retrieval
U2-L03 questioning about a familiar person; U2-L06 fresh person introduction; U7-L03 life-event profile; U9-L04 factual profile.

### Ledger load
**5 abilities / 6 phrases / 3 grammar records / 24 word records / 2 pronunciation records = 40 source introductions.**

Interpretation: the 24 word records are a semantic family network, not 24 equal active-recall targets in one sitting.

## U2-L02 — Which person is it?

### Primary performance
Understand a short simple description and identify the correct familiar person.

### Capability boundary
This lesson primarily serves **C08, a receptive capability**. Do not silently convert it into a full productive people-description lesson. Productive facts may be used as support, but the evidence task is identification from description.

### Foreground language
Description cues may combine:
- age/basic personal facts;
- family relationship;
- job;
- simple appearance/property adjectives;
- familiar clothing/colour cues where source-assigned.

Oxford phrases:
- `kind of sb/sth`
- `look like sb/sth`
- `this kind of…`

`look like` is the most naturally foregrounded people-description phrase. The `kind of / this kind of` language can remain more supported/receptive if the task does not require productive use.

### Grammar foreground
- adjective before noun;
- adjective after `be`;
- simple degree support such as `very` where useful;
- basic coordination of properties without turning this into a grammar lecture.

### Lexical handling
Twenty-four word records are assigned here across appearance/description/familiar-person vocabulary. Use contrasting person cards/images so the wider vocabulary earns meaning through identification, not list memorisation.

### Evidence task
The learner hears/reads a new short description and selects the correct person from plausible distractors, showing which cues led to the choice.

### Planned retrieval
Unit 3 object description changes the referent domain; U9-L04 returns to short factual descriptions; Unit 10 tests description recognition again with new content.

### Ledger load
**5 abilities / 3 phrases / 4 grammar records / 24 word records = 36 source introductions.**

## U2-L03 — Tell me about this person

### Primary performance
Ask and answer basic questions about a familiar person using information not already visible to both sides.

### Role in the unit
This is the interaction bridge between U2-L01 family knowledge and later transfer. It introduces no new top-level ability record because it practises and combines already required capabilities; that is intentional, not evidence that the lesson is empty.

### Foreground question functions
Use bounded questions about fields the learner already knows how to answer:
- who the person is / relationship;
- name/age;
- origin/residence;
- job/study;
- one or two familiar-person facts.

Reuse previously introduced wh-/yes-no patterns rather than opening a new general “question grammar” chapter.

### Phrase/expansion handling
The single source phrase assigned here should serve the information-gap task rather than becoming the lesson objective. X08 answer-expansion language may begin to appear in short natural answers where appropriate.

### Lexical-load warning
The ledger assigns **24 new word records** here even though the pedagogical purpose is interaction. Do not turn U2-L03 into a vocabulary lesson.

Use the words through:
- mystery-person cards;
- varied family/job/person profiles;
- recognition before production for less central items;
- repeated question-answer cycles where the words are needed to solve the task.

### Evidence task
Information gap: learner A and B have different information about the same/matched people. Success requires asking useful questions and answering from the available profile, not reading a prepared dialogue.

### Planned retrieval
U2-L06 fresh familiar-person profile; question initiation later in objects, directions, transactions, plans and exit work.

### Ledger load
**0 new ability records / 1 phrase / 0 grammar records / 24 word records = 25 source introductions.**

The low grammar/ability count and high lexical count are acceptable only if the authoring remains task-first.

## U2-L04 — What do you like doing?

### Primary performance
Understand and express basic likes, dislikes, hobbies, and personal interests.

### Foreground language
- `I like … / I don't like …`
- `I love … / I hate …` where source-supported and appropriate;
- `I enjoy …`
- `really like`
- `really enjoy`
- basic hobby/interest/media/activity vocabulary.

### Grammar foreground
The eight EGP rows here describe a compact preference system, not eight grammar lessons. Bound authoring to:
- preference verbs;
- simple affirmative/negative present forms;
- `really` with preference verbs;
- source-supported `like` + infinitive/`-ing` patterns only to the extent needed by the lesson examples.

Do not broaden this into unrestricted verb-pattern teaching.

### Important capability distinction
This is **C05 preference/interest**, not yet the fuller C22 opinion/evaluation capability. A learner saying `I really like tennis` is not the same as giving and supporting a general opinion. Unit 8 owns that expansion.

### Lexical handling
Twenty-four word records cover hobbies, media, activities and some evaluation/interest language. Foreground a usable subset that allows genuine choice; the rest can enter through a preference survey/menu and later routine/free-time recycling in Unit 4.

### Evidence task
With a new activity/media choice set, the learner states several likes/dislikes and understands another person's preferences well enough to match or compare profiles at a simple factual level.

### Planned retrieval
U2-L06 person profile; Unit 4 free time/media; Unit 5 food preferences; Unit 8 opinion/suggestion/planning.

### Ledger load
**5 abilities / 2 phrases / 8 grammar records / 24 word records = 39 source introductions.**

## U2-L05 — What can you do?

### Primary performance
Understand and state what the learner or another person can or cannot do in familiar activities.

### Foreground language
- `I can … / I can't …`
- `Can you …?`
- simple third-person ability statements where needed;
- `good at sth`
- `know how`
- `learn how to do sth` as a useful related chunk, without letting learning-process language displace the core ability task.

### Grammar foreground
The grammar records here belong to one bounded modal system:
- affirmative `can`;
- negative `can't`;
- yes/no question with `can`;
- `can` for ability;
- subject pronouns in modal questions.

### Critical boundary
Do **not** teach `can` for requests/offers here as if all modal meanings are one lesson. Unit 5 owns request/offer functions. Here `can` means ability/inability.

### Lexical handling
Twenty-four source words include activities/sport/music and general action vocabulary. Use an ability survey, person cards, or “who can do this?” task so vocabulary is needed to express real contrasts.

### Pronunciation
Even though no new pronunciation source strand is introduced in this slot, authoring should protect the meaning contrast between `can` and `can't` through clear contextual listening/production. Do not turn this into accent training.

### Evidence task
Fresh ability-grid exchange: ask about unfamiliar activities, understand the answer, and report what self/another person can or cannot do.

### Planned retrieval
U2-L06 integrated person profile; Unit 5 contrasts ability `can` with request/offer `can`; later practical tasks reuse the modal without changing this lesson's semantic boundary.

### Ledger load
**3 abilities / 3 phrases / 8 grammar records / 24 word records = 38 source introductions.**

## U2-L06 — Who's this? — Unit 2 Transfer

### Primary performance
Introduce and describe a **new familiar person** by integrating family/relationship information, simple identification cues, preferences/interests, and ability/inability.

### New-content boundary
The exact ledger introduces **0 new source records** here. That is intentional. This lesson is retrieval/transfer, not a disguised final teaching dump.

### Transfer design
Use a profile that the learner has not seen during teaching. A strong task requires a combination of:
1. understand a short description and identify/select the person;
2. ask for one or more missing facts;
3. explain the person's relationship/basic personal facts;
4. state one preference/interest;
5. state one ability/inability;
6. optionally introduce the person socially using Unit-1 language.

### Evidence modes
Use at least one receptive component and one productive/interactive component. Avoid a single monologue copied from a profile.

### Success criteria
The learner can combine Unit-2 language under a changed prompt while keeping family relationships, preference, ability and description meanings distinct enough to be understood.

### What this lesson verifies
Fresh Unit-2 evidence for **C03, C05, C07, C08**, while recycling C01/C02 and the relevant question/reference infrastructure.

### Planned later recycle
- C03 family/person information returns in past-life profiles U7 and factual descriptions U9;
- C05 interests return in Unit 4 and Unit 8;
- C07 `can` is contrasted with request/offer functions in Unit 5;
- C08 description/identification returns in Unit 3 and U9-L04.

### Ledger load
**0 abilities / 0 phrases / 0 grammar / 0 new words / 0 pronunciation = 0 source introductions.**

The zero is a positive design signal: the slot is reserved for genuine transfer.

## Unit 2 manual critic — applied

A redundant override was removed.
2. **C08 reception protected:** U2-L02 remains an identification-from-description lesson; productive description is not silently used to erase the receptive assessment boundary.
3. **U2-L03 lexical density controlled:** 24 word introductions are allowed only through information-gap/person-profile use, not a word-list chapter.
4. **U2-L04 grammar density collapsed into one preference system:** eight grammar records are not eight grammar explanations.
5. **`can` meanings separated:** U2-L05 owns ability/inability. Request/offer `can` waits for Unit 5.
6. **Transfer integrity protected:** U2-L06 introduces zero new source records and therefore functions as a clean retrieval/transfer checkpoint.
