This is the single Stage-07 **authoring contract**, not a lesson script. The exact source-record ledger remains authoritative for traceability/lifecycle.

## Global rules

1. Source record ≠ separate teaching object.
2. One primary learner performance per instructional lesson.
3. Foreground only language needed for the performance; other assigned records may enter as supported exposure.
4. Broad lexical sets = active core + supported recognition + later retrieval; no vocabulary dumps.
5. Multiple EGP rows may describe one usable system; no one-rule-one-slide syllabus.
6. Chunks before unnecessary grammar explanation.
7. Pronunciation attaches to actual use and prioritises intelligibility.
8. Transfer lessons introduce no hidden new system and change the prompt/context.
9. Mastery evidence cannot be copied from a complete model.
10. If real authoring cannot fit naturally, split/update the architecture rather than rush content.

Each brief records **performance → core/boundary → evidence → retrieval → load**.

# Unit 5 — Needs, Food, Shopping, and Simple Service

Purpose: immediate needs, requests, offers/invitations, food/drink, usable quantity, prices/payment, and food/shopping service transactions.  
Primary: **C18, food/shopping branches of C19, C11**, with C05 recycling.  
Main lexical homes: **L07, L08, L12, L16, L26**, with clothing/product support.  
Pronunciation: polite-request intonation, prominence of key information, chunk intelligibility.  
Transfer: **U5-L07**.

## U5-L01 — Can I have …? / I need …
**Performance:** express an immediate need and request a thing/help.  
**Core:** source phrase `ask for sth`, `excuse me`, request frames using source-supported `can`, needs/items/help vocabulary, polite opening/response recycling.  
**Grammar boundary:** grammar rows for `can` request, object/reference and modal infrastructure are one **request system**. Explicitly contrast meaning with Unit-2 ability `can`: same form family, different communicative function. Do not reteach ability.  
**Lexical handling:** 24 words must be selected through realistic immediate-need/request prompts, with only a manageable active core at once.  
**Pronunciation:** request/question contour and clear key-item stress.  
**Evidence:** unfamiliar immediate-need cards; learner initiates an appropriate request and understands the response.  
**Retrieval:** offer/service L02/L03, shopping L06, directions/help Unit 6.  
**Load:** **4 / 2 / 4 / 24 / 1 = 35**.

## U5-L02 — Would you like …?
**Performance:** make/respond to a basic offer or simple invitation.  
**Core:** `would like`, `Would you like…?`, `No problem`, `of course`, source-supported `would like to invite`, plus simple acceptance/refusal/offer response as required by abilities.  
**Grammar boundary:** five EGP records form one bounded `would like`/offer/invitation system. Do not present `would` as a general tense/modal chapter. Keep `can` offers only where natural and clearly functional.  
**Capability boundary:** C18 owns request/offer/invitation; food ordering later recycles `would like` but does not redefine it.  
**Evidence:** role-switch mini-scenarios where learner must both initiate and respond; no fixed dialogue memorisation.  
**Retrieval:** food service L03, social planning Unit 8.  
**Load:** **3 / 4 / 5 / 23 / 1 = 36**.

## U5-L03 — I'd like …, please

**Boundary:** 24 food words must become menu choices/categories and recognition first—not a 24-item food quiz. Zero new grammar is positive: the lesson applies L01/L02 interaction systems to a real service branch.  
**Evidence:** unfamiliar simple menu; learner orders a small set of items, handles `anything else?`, and closes politely.  
**Retrieval:** quantity L04, price/payment L05, mixed café/shop transfer L07.
