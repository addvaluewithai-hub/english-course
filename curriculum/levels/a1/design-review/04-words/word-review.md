## Core decision

The A1 word inventory does **not** force another top-level communicative capability.

The lexical inventory is broad, but the broad areas that are not themselves capabilities can be taught as **required lexical/content sets inside existing capabilities and tasks**. Creating a new capability merely because a topic has many nouns would confuse content with learner performance.

Examples:
- body/health vocabulary can live inside wellbeing, description, needs/help, simple instructions and service contexts;
- clothes/appearance can live inside object/person description and shopping;
- animals/nature can live inside receptive descriptions, object description, preferences and simple factual language;
- school/learning can live inside personal information, routines, clarification and writing tasks;
- media/technology can live inside interests, routines, personal messages and contact-information tasks.

A word having a natural home in a phrase, grammar structure, or capability task means **no separate lexical lesson is required for that word**. It does **not** mean the word may appear once and disappear. Later lesson mapping must still provide introduction/use, fresh retrieval, recycling, and verification.

## Source-hygiene convention

The canonical word inventory remains `words.json`. This review is a **teaching-home architecture**, not a second copy of the source ledger.

- Single-word items named below are lexical content homes.
- Multiword expressions such as `best friend`, `get married`, `phone number`, `work as sth`, etc. are **phrase-stage cross-links** where useful; they do not count as extra word records.
- Grammar frames such as `there is/there are` are grammar/phrase cross-links, not extra lexical records.
- The same word may appear in more than one semantic set because reuse is desirable; this file is not a one-record-one-row accounting table.
- Exact source-record coverage later must use the IDs in `words.json`, especially where Oxford distinguishes multiple records/senses for the same surface form (for example `like` or `second`).

# Lexical-system boundaries

## 1. Word-level CEFR placement does not equal full grammar-system mastery

Oxford places several grammatical/function words at A1 that do not correspond to a full productive A1 EGP system in the canonical grammar inventory. They remain A1 lexical/form content, but the course must not silently overclaim grammar mastery.

Important examples:
- `have to`
- `must`
- `should`
- `could`
- `if`
- `than`
- `however`

Treatment:
- teach their A1 source-supported lexical/fixed/basic uses where natural;
- allow receptive/fixed-expression use where appropriate;
- do not infer a broad obligation/advice/conditional/comparative/discourse grammar system unless canonical grammar evidence supports it;
- later lesson authoring must respect the boundaries already recorded for comparison and future grammar.

## 2. Comparison remains limited at A1

The lexical list includes `better`, `best`, `compare`, `difference`, `different`, `similar`, `same`, `than`, `more`, and `most`.

These words justify **lexical comparison language/exposure**, not a full A1 comparative-grammar capability. This confirms X09 from the grammar review. Productive comparative-system control remains bounded by the grammar source progression.

## 3. Grammar/function vocabulary is primarily owned by X10 and capability grammar

Items such as articles, pronouns, auxiliaries/modals, basic determiners, conjunctions, question words and common prepositions do not need artificial lexical-topic sets.

Examples include:
`a/an`, `the`, `I`, `you`, `he`, `she`, `it`, `we`, `they`, `me`, `him`, `her`, `us`, `them`, `my`, `your`, `his`, `its`, `our`, `their`, `be`, `do`, `can`, `will`, `would`, `and`, `but`, `or`, `because`, `what`, `when`, `where`, `which`, `who`, `why`, `how`, `in`, `on`, `at`, `to`, `from`, `for`, `with`, `without`, `of`, `by`, `under`, `over`, `between`, `through`, `into`.

Their lexical presence is accounted for through grammar/capability use, not by making a “function words lesson.”

# Critic resolution

The critic pass challenged the lexical architecture rather than accepting the first grouping automatically.

1. **Body/health stays a content set.** The lexical density is real, but there is no matching A1 ability evidence for a medical-service capability. Do not manufacture one.
2. **Jobs/work stays one set with two internal subgroups.** Occupations and workplace language share personal-information/routine homes and do not need architectural separation.
3. **School/learning and text/writing remain separate conceptual sets with deliberate overlap.** One is a life domain; the other is task/product vocabulary.
4. **Animals/nature and weather remain separate.** Weather has explicit ability/content evidence; animals/nature mainly feeds description/factual language.
5. **Advice/obligation/conditional/comparison words do not create new capabilities or grammar contracts by themselves.** Lexical placement is not enough evidence for productive system mastery.
6. **Phrase/grammar cross-links are support, not word records.** This distinction is now explicit throughout the file.
7. **No exact source record is deleted by lexical grouping.** `words.json` remains the canonical 900-record ledger; later coverage validation must join by source record ID rather than trusting headword strings or these semantic example lists.
