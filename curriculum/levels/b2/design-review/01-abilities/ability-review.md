## Capability architecture

### Reception — reading

**C01 — Read arguments, viewpoints and author stance**  
Identify claims, conclusions, contrasting positions, purpose, audience, fact/opinion, persuasion, tone and other evidence needed to interpret an author’s stance in extended or complex written texts.

**C02 — Locate, integrate and evaluate information across written sources**  
Scan long texts, identify key/supporting information, integrate text with visual information, synthesize across sources, and evaluate summaries or source relationships.

**C03 — Follow complex functional, technical and instructional texts**  
Understand long instructions, specifications, advice, correspondence, questionnaires and specialised factual material with appropriate rereading/reference support.

**C04 — Infer meaning and interpret narrative/pragmatic nuance in writing**  
Infer likely development, attitude and implied meaning; interpret narrative development, irony, double meaning and context-dependent lexical nuance without turning these into unrestricted literary-analysis claims.

### Reception — listening

**C05 — Follow extended spoken input across media, presentations and conversation**  
Track main points, details, sequence, topic movement and complex information in natural-speed conversation, broadcasts, presentations and other extended standard-language input.

**C06 — Interpret spoken argument, viewpoint, attitude and pragmatic intent**  
Identify reasons for/against, speaker viewpoint, fact/opinion, tone, assumptions, rhetorical intent, irony and other stance signals in discussion and presentation.

**C07 — Follow detailed spoken instructions, processes and advice**  
Understand detailed instructions, multi-stage processes and domain-relevant advice accurately enough to act on the information.

### Spoken production

**C08 — Give clear, detailed spoken descriptions, narratives, explanations and procedures**  
Describe people, places, products, experiences and processes; narrate events; provide factual/technical information; and give clear instructions or informative presentations with relevant detail.

**C09 — Present, justify and evaluate viewpoints orally**  
Explain solutions, recommend action, compare/evaluate options, develop reasoned argument, support claims with reasons/examples, and present structured viewpoints at B2 depth.

**C10 — Express probability, speculation, causes, consequences and hypotheticals orally**  
Use bounded B2 language to discuss past/future possibility, assumptions, hypothetical situations, causes and potential consequences without treating one form as unrestricted system mastery.

### Oral interaction

**C11 — Sustain and manage collaborative discussion/conversation**  
Exchange information and views, participate fluently, introduce or redirect perspectives, respond to others, manage participation and develop a discussion while preserving the source mode of each mapped component.

**C12 — Handle oral problems, complaints, negotiation and resolution**  
Describe transactional problems, request or demand appropriate action, handle less-routine service situations, negotiate perspectives and resolve disputes. Production and interaction source modes remain distinct subcomponents inside this family.

**C13 — Participate in formal discussion, interviews, surveys and presentation Q&A**  
Exchange information in formal settings, ask/answer detailed questions, handle presentation questions, conduct/follow up interviews and contribute appropriately in formal discussion.

### Mediation / relay

**C14 — Relay, paraphrase, summarize and facilitate information/ideas**  
Pass on information reliably, paraphrase, summarize spoken/written material, record key points where the source mode licenses it, and facilitate shared understanding. Cross-links from reception remain reception unless the original source record itself is mediation/production.

### Written production and interaction

**C15 — Produce detailed descriptive, explanatory, procedural and narrative writing**  
Write clear detailed factual descriptions, explanations, narratives, procedures and complex-process accounts with appropriate supporting detail.

**C16 — Build written argument, evaluation and source-informed synthesis**  
Compare/evaluate ideas, distinguish and connect claims/counterclaims, synthesize information, develop supported arguments, challenge evidence and represent contrasting viewpoints coherently.

**C17 — Manage written correspondence and audience relationship**  
Write personal/formal correspondence, invitations, applications, complaints and rapport-building messages with purpose-appropriate conventions, detail and register.

**C18 — Produce extended functional genres: reports and reviews**  
Write structured reports/reviews and problem/solution or recommendation texts where text-type conventions and global organization create a distinct performance boundary beyond sentence-level exposition or argument.

## Cross-cutting requirements

These requirements attach across capabilities and **must not become standalone units merely because they have source rows**.

1. **Source/reference and lexical strategy** — dictionaries/reference materials, contextual word inference, source attribution and efficient checking.
2. **Discourse cohesion and information structure** — cohesive devices, linking, main/supporting detail, paragraph logic, substitution/paraphrase tracking and long-turn/text organization.
3. **Register, audience and formality** — audience awareness, genre-appropriate formality, formal/informal shifts and recipient-sensitive planning.
4. **Pragmatic/rhetorical nuance and stance** — irony, hyperbole, rhetorical questions, connotation, tact, enthusiasm/reluctance and other bounded pragmatic meanings.
5. **Repair, paraphrase and circumlocution** — correcting misunderstanding, reformulating, lexical substitution and covering gaps without equating the strategy with broad productive mastery.
6. **Fluency and turn management** — gaining time, maintaining a turn, entering/redirecting discussion and sustaining participation at B2 tempo.
7. **Metacognitive monitoring, editing and accuracy** — planning, noticing recurring errors, concise editing, and checking spelling/punctuation/grammar in long texts.
8. **Prosody as communicative meaning** — intonation/certainty evidence is retained here for Stage 4.5 pronunciation integration rather than treated as an isolated accent target.
9. **Genre/layout conventions** — headings, subheadings, sections and genre organization support the relevant writing capability rather than becoming independent capabilities.

## Modality safeguards and critic decisions

- Reception records remain reception even where the source statement names a response activity. For example, aid rows **17, 29 and 68** may cross-link to summary/discussion work, but their source reception boundary is retained.

- Interaction records retain an exchange/response requirement rather than being assessed as solo sentence production.
- Production-coded records that contain interaction-like language retain their source mode in the exact source trace; capability grouping does not rewrite the source classification.
- Cross-cutting discourse, repair, register, rhetoric, editing and strategy requirements are not promoted to fake capabilities.
- No topic/vocabulary theme was used to create a capability family.
- No unit or lesson count was inferred.
- No B1 record or locked B1 decision was changed.
