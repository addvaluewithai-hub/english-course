## Productive vs receptive treatment — Stage-4 decision boundary

Stage 4 establishes **likely treatment**, not the Stage-8 final semantic/control role.

- **Active-core leaning:** high-utility argument/evaluation, interaction/negotiation, change/process, data/trend, logical-relation, probability and problem-solving vocabulary (especially L03/L04/L18/L20/L22/L23/L24), plus selected common items from other sets.
- **Mixed active + supported recognition:** work/education, feelings, media/source handling, administration, business, technology, travel and formal-register sets.
- **Recognition-heavy breadth:** specialist/public-domain vocabulary in law/conflict, health, environment/science, society, arts/culture and some technical domains unless later lesson evidence justifies productive control.
- **Grammar-owned:** L27 and any lexical record whose usable control is better represented by a Stage-3 grammar system.
- **General remainder:** contextual reuse without forcing a false semantic theme.

Final item-level productive/receptive/grammar-owned roles remain deliberately unresolved until Stage 8.

### Stage-2 phrase polysemy carry-forward

The phrase review flagged surface strings such as `account for`, `appeal to`, `back ... up`, `bring ... up`, `figure ... out`, `go off`, `go through`, `set out`, `take advantage of` and `take over` as sense-sensitive.

Stage 4 therefore applies this rule: **a base-word record never inherits every phrasal/idiomatic sense merely because the surface word matches**. Where a B2 word record overlaps a phrase surface (`account`, `appeal`, `back`, `figure`, etc.), the exact source record and phrase frame remain separate evidence until Stage 6/8 reconciliation.
