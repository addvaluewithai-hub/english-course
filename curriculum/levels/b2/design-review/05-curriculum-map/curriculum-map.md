# B2 Curriculum Map — Stage-05 Candidate Skeleton

Status: **AI-ASSISTED CURRICULUM-MAP CANDIDATE — OWNER HUMAN APPROVAL PENDING; BASELINE FOR STAGE 06 EXACT LEDGER**  
Level: **B2**  
Reviewed candidate architecture entering Stage 5: **18 capability families + cross-cutting requirements + 47 grammar systems + 28 lexical/content homes + LX-GENERAL + 10 pronunciation strands**  
Baseline shape emerging from dependency/load mapping: **13 units / 95 lesson slots**

This map is the first B2 stage allowed to propose unit and lesson-slot architecture. The shape was derived after Stage 1–4.5 review of abilities, phrases, grammar, lexical content, pronunciation, cumulative exit constraints and locked B1 carry-forward. It does **not** copy B1's 12-unit / 85-slot architecture and no target B2 count was supplied before mapping.

The **95-slot** baseline emerged from keeping B2 reception, production, interaction, mediation and writing performance boundaries independently teachable while protecting later fresh retrieval, the three explicit B2 assessment requirements, cumulative pronunciation/fluency evidence, and zero-new transfer slots. Stage 06 exact source-record/load/lifecycle work may still move, merge or split slots when evidence requires it.

## Shape accounting

- **13 units**
- **95 lesson slots total**
- **77 instructional / focused-retrieval slots**
- **12 unit-end fresh-transfer slots** (Units 1–12)
- **6 final integration / exit-evidence slots** (Unit 13)
- designated zero-new slots: **18**
- unit sizes vary from **6 to 8** slots because performance complexity and dependency load vary

## Mapping rules

1. Each instructional lesson has one primary communicative/evidence outcome; integrated skills are allowed only when they coherently serve that outcome.
2. B1 language/performance is prerequisite retrieval, not reclassified as new B2 inventory.
3. Grammar systems serve performance; there is no one-EGP-row-one-lesson architecture.
4. The 1,427 B2 lexical records provide content/range evidence, not 1,427 productive teaching objectives and not topic-vocabulary units.
5. Pronunciation is distributed inside real reception/production/interaction/mediation; no standalone accent unit is created.
6. C01–C07 complex reception stays independently evidenced; it cannot be replaced by speaking/writing output.
7. C11–C13 interaction requires real exchange/change, not solo speaking disguised as interaction.
8. C14 mediation requires source transformation/facilitation; comprehension questions alone do not satisfy it.
9. X-DISCOURSE, X-REFERENCE, X-REGISTER, X-STANCE, X-PRAGMATICS, X-REPAIR, X-FLUENCY-TURN and editing/genre requirements recur across the map.
10. Every designated transfer/final-integration slot has **zero planned new required B2 source introductions**. Stage 06 must enforce this exactly.
11. B2 assessment paths are spaced: complex-input interpretation, spontaneous contribution, and supported viewpoint for a new audience/topic each receive development plus later changed-context evidence.
12. If Stage 06 exact load/lifecycle evidence contradicts this skeleton, reopen the map rather than hide records in transfer/review lessons.

# Unit 1 — Read Viewpoints, Arguments, and Persuasive Stance
**7 slots**  
Primary capability: **C01**; cross-links C02/C04.  
Cross-cutting: X-DISCOURSE, X-STANCE, X-PRAGMATICS, X-REFERENCE, X-LEXICAL-STRATEGY.  
B1 carry-forward: CF01/CF12/CF13.  
Grammar/resources: G08/G13/G16/G21/G30 plus bounded comparison/evaluation resources.  
Pronunciation: not a primary production target; source/audio cross-links may support later prosody work.

Purpose: move from B1 straightforward viewpoint recognition to B2 interpretation of claims, evidence, audience, persuasion, contrast, rhetorical effect and author stance in longer written texts.

### U1-L01 — Recover the argument structure in an extended text
Identify central claim, supporting reasons/evidence, examples and conclusion without treating every paragraph as equally important.

### U1-L02 — Distinguish fact, claim, inference and evaluation
Track what is presented as evidence versus interpretation/opinion and identify where the writer moves between them.

### U1-L03 — Compare contrasting positions inside one text
Follow concession, counter-position and rebuttal; use discourse/reference signals to maintain who believes what.

### U1-L04 — Identify purpose, audience and persuasive choices
Interpret how selection, emphasis, rhetorical questions, framing or tone serve a likely audience/purpose.

### U1-L05 — Infer stance, attitude and bounded rhetorical nuance
Use context to interpret irony, emphasis, scepticism, enthusiasm or distancing where source evidence supports it; avoid unrestricted literary-analysis claims.

### U1-L06 — Evaluate the strength/relevance of support
Judge whether a stated reason/example supports the identified claim sufficiently for the reading task, without turning the lesson into formal logic training.

### U1-L07 — Fresh written-argument interpretation transfer
Unseen article/report/opinion text: map claim → support → counter-position → stance/purpose and answer a new evidence question. **Zero planned new required B2 content.**

**Unit critic:** C01 remains reception. Learner opinion production may follow as a task, but it does not replace the reading evidence.

# Unit 2 — Integrate Complex Written Information, Instructions, and Implied Meaning
**7 slots**  
Primary capabilities: **C02, C03, C04**.  
Cross-cutting: X-REFERENCE, X-DISCOURSE, X-LEXICAL-STRATEGY, X-REGISTER.  
B1 carry-forward: CF01/CF12/CF14.  
Grammar/resources: G10/G31/G32/G38 and reference/information-density systems.

Purpose: build the other three B2 reading boundaries: integration across sources/visuals, long functional/technical texts, and inference/narrative-pragmatic nuance.

### U2-L01 — Locate and integrate information across a long text
Use headings, structure, reference and strategic rereading to combine separated pieces of information for one task.

### U2-L02 — Integrate text with tables, diagrams or visual information
Resolve agreement/contrast between prose and visual data and select information relevant to a new question.

### U2-L03 — Compare and synthesize two written sources
Identify shared facts, differences, source emphasis and compatible/conflicting information without yet requiring full written synthesis production.

### U2-L04 — Follow long functional, technical or procedural writing
Track conditions, sequence, warnings, specifications or advice in detailed instructions and field-related factual material with permitted reference support.

### U2-L05 — Handle detailed correspondence, forms and questionnaires receptively
Interpret purpose, requirements, implied action and register in longer practical/work/education correspondence or structured documents.

### U2-L06 — Infer development, implied meaning and contextual nuance
Interpret likely development, context-dependent lexical meaning, double meaning or narrative/pragmatic implication where evidence permits.

### U2-L07 — Fresh complex-written-input transfer
New multi-source pack: locate → integrate → infer → act on one instruction/requirement → explain source relationship. **Zero planned new required B2 content.**

**Unit critic:** C02 information integration, C03 functional/technical reading and C04 inference/narrative nuance remain separately visible even though one transfer task may integrate them.

# Unit 3 — Follow Extended Spoken Input, Argument, and Detailed Advice
**7 slots**  
Primary capabilities: **C05, C06, C07**.  
Cross-cutting: X-DISCOURSE, X-STANCE, X-PRAGMATICS, X-REFERENCE.  
Pronunciation emphasis: **P01, P07, P08, P09** receptive evidence.  
B1 carry-forward: CF01/CF16.  
Exit path: repeated complex-input interpretation.

Purpose: extend B1 familiar factual listening into longer natural-speed standard-language input, spoken argument/stance and detailed multi-stage instructions/advice.

### U3-L01 — Track main points and topic movement in an extended talk
Follow a longer presentation/conversation without transcript dependence, using discourse and prominence cues to maintain the information map.

### U3-L02 — Recover important detail in natural-speed standard input
Identify selected supporting facts, examples, sequence or change while tolerating some unfamiliar language through context and lexical strategy.

### U3-L03 — Follow standard-language news, documentary or film-related input
Track main event/message, relevant detail and speaker/source framing in authentic-style but source-bounded material.

### U3-L04 — Follow reasons for and against in spoken argument
Identify speaker claims, supporting reasons, counter-position and conclusion across an extended discussion/presentation.

### U3-L05 — Interpret attitude, certainty and rhetorical intent
Use wording plus prosodic evidence to distinguish confidence/uncertainty, agreement/reservation, irony or rhetorical emphasis where clearly supported.

### U3-L06 — Follow detailed instructions, processes and field-related advice
Track multi-stage spoken guidance accurately enough to select/perform the required action or explain the procedure.

### U3-L07 — Fresh extended-listening transfer
New speaker/topic: follow extended input → map main/supporting information → identify stance or instruction → respond to a source-based task. **Zero planned new required B2 content.**

**Unit critic:** transcription is never the success condition; C05/C06/C07 remain reception and feed later production/mediation without being replaced by it.

# Unit 4 — Give Clear Detailed Explanations, Narratives, and Procedures
**8 slots**  
Primary capability: **C08**; cross-links C13.  
Cross-cutting: X-DISCOURSE, X-REFERENCE, X-METACOGNITION, X-FLUENCY-TURN.  
Pronunciation: **P02–P08, P10** distributed.  
B1 carry-forward: CF05/CF06/CF12/CF14/CF16.  
Grammar/resources: G01/G03/G05/G10/G11/G18/G19/G31/G32/G34–G37.

Purpose: move from B1 connected explanation/narrative to B2 clear, detailed, better-organised extended spoken production with denser reference and sustained intelligibility.

### U4-L01 — Build a clear detailed description with information hierarchy
Describe a person/place/product/situation with selected detail, precise reference and a clear main/supporting-information structure.

### U4-L02 — Explain a process or procedure for another person
Organise stages, conditions, cause/result and warnings so the listener can reconstruct or act on the process.

### U4-L03 — Give a detailed factual or field-related explanation
Use broader lexical range and complex noun/reference structures to explain familiar field-related information without pretending specialist expertise.

### U4-L04 — Narrate events with background, prior time and consequence
Retrieve B1 narrative control and extend it with richer background, viewpoint, prior sequence and outcome while keeping chronology intelligible.

### U4-L05 — Explain change, trend and development over time
Use comparison, degree, aspect/viewpoint and cause/effect resources to explain how a situation developed and why it matters.

### U4-L06 — Organise a longer informative presentation from notes
Plan sections/main points/supporting examples and speak from concise notes rather than a full script.

### U4-L07 — Sustain intelligibility and information structure over a longer turn
Integrate word stress, prominence, chunking, grammatical endings and repair so accent has little effect on understanding.

### U4-L08 — Fresh detailed-speaking transfer
New familiar/field-related prompt: plan briefly → give a detailed explanation/narrative/procedure → handle one clarification request. **Zero planned new required B2 content.**

**Unit critic:** this unit develops C08 depth; it does not absorb viewpoint argument (C09), formal Q&A (C13) or mediation (C14).

# Unit 5 — Support Viewpoints, Evaluate Options, and Speculate
**8 slots**  
Primary capabilities: **C09, C10**.  
Cross-cutting: X-STANCE, X-DISCOURSE, X-REGISTER, X-REFERENCE.  
Pronunciation: P07/P09/P10.  
B1 carry-forward: CF03/CF12/CF13/CF14.  
Grammar/resources: G02/G04/G07/G12/G14/G21–G30, plus bounded future/past-hypothesis systems.  
Exit path: first explicit supported-viewpoint transfer.

Purpose: build B2 oral argument/evaluation together with the distinct probability/speculation/hypothetical resources needed to discuss uncertainty, causes and consequences.

### U5-L01 — State and develop a position with reasons and examples
Move beyond B1 brief justification: make a claim, sequence supporting reasons and add relevant examples/detail.

### U5-L02 — Compare advantages, disadvantages and criteria
Evaluate alternatives using nuanced comparison/degree language and explicit criteria rather than a list of adjectives.

### U5-L03 — Recommend action and justify priorities
Choose among alternatives, explain trade-offs and show why one criterion or consequence matters more in context.

### U5-L04 — Explain causes, consequences and linked change
Develop causal reasoning using bounded condition/consequence and correlative comparison resources.

### U5-L05 — Express probability, expectation and deduction
Distinguish likelihood, certainty, expectation, hearsay or deduction using source-bounded modal/semi-modal resources.

### U5-L06 — Speculate about past alternatives, causes and missed possibilities
Use bounded perfect-modal/past-viewpoint resources for possibility, regret or speculation without implying unrestricted perfect-modal mastery.

### U5-L07 — Discuss hypothetical alternatives and consequences
Use sourced conditional/hypothetical structures to explore a counterfactual or imagined option while maintaining a real communicative purpose.

### U5-L08 — Fresh supported-viewpoint transfer — first major evidence
New topic plus changed audience role: interpret brief input → take a position → evaluate options → support with reasons/examples → respond to one challenge. **Zero planned new required B2 content.**

**Unit critic:** C09 evaluation/argument and C10 speculation remain distinct success conditions; shared grammar does not collapse them into one modal/conditional chapter.

# Unit 6 — Sustain Collaborative Discussion with Useful Spontaneity
**7 slots**  
Primary capability: **C11**.  
Cross-cutting: X-FLUENCY-TURN, X-PRAGMATICS, X-STANCE, X-REPAIR.  
Pronunciation: P07/P08/P09/P10.  
B1 carry-forward: CF02/CF03/CF04/CF13/CF16.  
Exit path: spontaneous contribution.

Purpose: turn B1 unprepared familiar conversation into B2 collaborative discussion that can sustain, redirect and develop ideas with a fairly even tempo.

### U6-L01 — Enter and sustain a multi-turn discussion
Contribute relevantly without waiting for scripted questions; connect to the prior turn and maintain the shared topic.

### U6-L02 — Develop another participant's idea
Add support, qualification, example or consequence to someone else's point rather than simply giving a separate opinion.

### U6-L03 — Redirect or introduce a new perspective coherently
Shift angle while signalling the relationship to the current discussion and preserving interactional coherence.

### U6-L04 — Agree, partially agree, disagree and hedge with tact
Use stance/pragmatic/prosodic resources to manage disagreement without losing clarity or social appropriacy.

### U6-L05 — Repair misunderstanding while keeping the discussion moving
Reformulate, clarify, check interpretation and cover lexical gaps with minimal disruption to the shared task.

### U6-L06 — Make a spontaneous contribution under changing information
Respond to an unexpected new fact/position with relevant reasoning and no full script/model; maintain intelligibility and turn flow.

### U6-L07 — Fresh collaborative-discussion transfer
New participants/issue/role cards: sustain discussion → develop others' ideas → handle disagreement → respond spontaneously to a changed condition. **Zero planned new required B2 content.**

**Unit critic:** spontaneous contribution is interaction evidence, not a solo monologue; normal monitoring is allowed, but complete scripting is not.

Primary capability: **C12**; cross-links C09/C11.

### U7-L01 — Describe a problem precisely and establish responsibility/context
State what happened, relevant conditions/evidence and the practical consequence without overloading irrelevant detail.

### U7-L05 — Handle disagreement, blame and tactful challenge

### U7-L06 — Resolve a less-routine service/work/travel dispute
Integrate explanation, negotiation, repair and agreement on next action in a genuinely changing interaction.

### U7-L07 — Fresh negotiation/problem-resolution transfer

# Unit 8 — Formal Discussion, Interviews, Surveys, and Presentation Q&A
**7 slots**  
Primary capability: **C13**; cross-links C08/C09/C11.  
Cross-cutting: X-REGISTER, X-FLUENCY-TURN, X-PRAGMATICS, X-REFERENCE.  
Pronunciation: P02–P10, strongest P06/P07/P09/P10.  
B1 carry-forward: CF06/CF08/CF13/CF16.

Purpose: build audience/formality-sensitive questioning and response in presentations, interviews, surveys and formal discussion.

### U8-L01 — Ask detailed, purposeful questions in a formal setting
Form questions that elicit evidence, explanation, clarification or follow-up rather than collecting disconnected facts.

### U8-L02 — Give developed answers with relevant detail
Answer formal questions directly, then add support/example/qualification while avoiding memorised speech blocks.

### U8-L03 — Conduct and follow up an interview or survey
Plan question sequence, listen to the response and choose a relevant spontaneous follow-up based on what was actually said.

### U8-L04 — Participate in a structured formal discussion
Enter at an appropriate point, refer to previous contributions, state a position and maintain register across disagreement.

### U8-L05 — Deliver a viewpoint/informative presentation for questions
Present enough structure/evidence for an audience to challenge or inquire; pronunciation/prosody should make information hierarchy clear.

### U8-L06 — Handle presentation questions and challenge spontaneously
Clarify the question, answer, acknowledge uncertainty where appropriate and reformulate when the first answer is unclear.

### U8-L07 — Fresh formal-interaction transfer
New role/topic: short presentation or briefing → formal Q&A/interview/discussion → spontaneous follow-up and repair. **Zero planned new required B2 content.**

# Unit 9 — Mediate, Relay, Summarize, and Connect Ideas
**8 slots**  
Primary capability: **C14**; cross-links C01–C07/C11.  
Cross-cutting: X-DISCOURSE, X-REFERENCE, X-REPAIR, X-REGISTER, X-STANCE.  
Pronunciation: P01/P06–P10 for spoken relay/facilitation.  
B1 carry-forward: CF09/CF04/CF12/CF16.  
Grammar/resources: G11/G16/G29/G32/G35 and reporting/source-reference systems.

Purpose: extend B1 summary/reporting into B2 selection, reliable relay, paraphrase, synthesis and facilitation of shared understanding.

### U9-L01 — Select information for a new recipient/purpose
Decide what a recipient needs from a longer source and exclude detail that is accurate but irrelevant to the mediation goal.

### U9-L02 — Paraphrase a complex point without changing its meaning
Re-express source wording/structure using bounded lexical/grammar resources while preserving important qualification and stance.

### U9-L03 — Summarize an extended written or spoken source
Compress main claim/information and essential support without copying source sequence sentence by sentence.

### U9-L04 — Relay detailed argument and distinguish source positions
Report who claims what, reasons/evidence and relevant reservations while keeping mediator versus source stance clear.

### U9-L05 — Integrate and compare information from multiple sources
Synthesize compatible points, flag differences/uncertainty and build a recipient-oriented account.

### U9-L06 — Clarify a complex point for another participant
Notice likely misunderstanding, explain/reframe the issue and check whether shared understanding has improved.

### U9-L07 — Connect and develop others' ideas in facilitation
Summarize a contribution, show relationship to another idea and invite/enable a next step without taking over the discussion.

### U9-L08 — Fresh mediation mission
New sources + recipient/group need: select → paraphrase/summarize → relay positions → clarify one misunderstanding → connect ideas. **Zero planned new required B2 content.**

# Unit 13 — B2 Integration and Exit Evidence
**6 slots — all zero-new**  
Purpose: cumulative fresh evidence with changed topics/audiences/speakers and reduced model dependence. These slots introduce **no planned new required B2 source record/system/lexical target**.

### U13-L01 — Complex-input interpretation mission
New multi-modal source pack (extended reading + listening): identify argument/information structure, integrate relevant details, infer stance/implication and act on one complex instruction/decision. Primary assessment path for **b2.assessment.01**.

### U13-L02 — Spontaneous collaborative contribution
New discussion with changing information/roles: sustain interaction, develop another idea, disagree/qualify, repair misunderstanding and contribute relevantly without a full script. Primary assessment path for **b2.assessment.02**.

### U13-L03 — Supported viewpoint for a new audience and topic
Unpractised topic + changed audience/context: interpret brief source input, formulate a position, support it with reasons/evidence, address a counter-position and adapt register. May be delivered orally or in writing with the complementary mode sampled elsewhere. Primary assessment path for **b2.assessment.03**.

### U13-L04 — Fresh mediation and facilitation mission
New detailed sources + recipient/group need: select/synthesize, paraphrase/relay arguments, clarify a complex point and connect participant ideas while preserving source stance.

### U13-L05 — Extended genre production under a new brief
Produce a clear detailed text in a genre not identical to the learner's most recent practice—argument/report/review/explanation/correspondence branch—with logical paragraphs, reference, register and self-editing.

### U13-L06 — Integrated B2 communication capstone
Changed scenario combining complex input → collaborative decision/problem → short spoken explanation/viewpoint → mediated relay or written follow-up. Evidence samples range, grammar control, discourse, pronunciation, fluency, interaction and cumulative B1 retention without turning one task into the sole proof of every claim.

**Unit critic:** Unit 13 is evidence, not a storage place for leftover inventory. Stage 06 must fail the map if any new required B2 record is first introduced in these six slots.

# Whole-map dependency critic

## 1. Complex reception precedes dependent production and mediation
Units 1–3 establish/retrieve the B2 reading/listening boundaries before source-heavy argument, formal interaction and mediation become major production demands. Later units still recycle reception with new sources.

## 2. Detailed speaking precedes high-pressure interaction
Unit 4 develops longer organised turns and sustained intelligibility before Units 6–8 demand spontaneous discussion, negotiation and formal Q&A.

## 3. Viewpoint depth precedes discussion transfer
Unit 5 develops supported argument/evaluation/speculation before Unit 6 collaborative discussion and Unit 7 negotiation require those resources under interaction pressure.

## 4. Spontaneity is spaced, not tested once
Unit 6 gives the first major spontaneous-contribution path; Units 7–9 and Unit 8 formal Q&A reuse changed-information interaction; U13-L02 is later fresh verification.

## 5. Supported-viewpoint transfer is spaced across modes
U5-L08 supplies a first changed-topic/audience oral transfer, U11-L08 supplies source-informed written transfer, and U13-L03 changes audience/topic again for final cumulative evidence.

## 6. Mediation has prerequisite source work and later retrieval
Units 1–3 prepare complex source interpretation; Unit 9 explicitly teaches source transformation/facilitation; Units 11–12 reuse source/audience decisions; U13-L04 retrieves mediation under a new brief.

## 7. Writing is distributed by performance boundary
C15 detailed exposition/narrative lives in Unit 10; C16 argument/evaluation/synthesis in Unit 11; C17 correspondence/online collaboration and C18 reports/reviews in Unit 12. No single generic writing unit hides modality/genre differences.

## 8. Grammar is dependency-led, not chapter-led
Reference/information-density resources recur early in reception and later in output; comparison/stance precede argument; modal/hypothetical resources serve U5/U6/U7; passive/reporting systems support technical input, mediation and formal writing; narrative/aspect systems extend retained B1 control inside U4/U10 rather than restarting tense teaching.

## 9. Lexical breadth is distributed
