# A2 Curriculum Map — Manual Stage-05 Skeleton

Status: **STAGE 05 COMPLETE — BASELINE MAP FOR EXACT LEDGER; NOT A FIXED LESSON COUNT**  
Level: **A2**  
Current architecture: **26 capabilities + 9 cross-cutting requirements**  
Baseline shape emerging from manual mapping: **12 units / 83 lesson slots**

This map was built manually from the reviewed A2 abilities, phrases, grammar systems, lexical/content sets, pronunciation, A1 carry-forward and A2 exit constraints. It does **not** copy A1's 10-unit/62-lesson shape. The 83-slot count is provisional: Stage 06 exact source-record/load/lifecycle evidence may split, merge or move slots.

## Mapping rules

1. Each lesson has one primary communicative/evidence outcome; integrated skills are allowed when they serve that outcome.
2. Grammar/phrases/words serve the performance; they do not define the unit order by themselves.
3. Unit-end transfer uses changed context/fresh evidence, not repetition of the same model.
4. A1 language enters as prerequisite/retrieval, not as new A2 inventory.
5. C20 repair, X02 linked output, X06 cohesion, X09 reference-building and pronunciation recur throughout the course.
6. No lesson is allowed to become a topic-vocabulary dump merely because an A2 lexical set is large.
7. Exact item introduction/retrieval/recycle/exit ownership comes in Stage 06.

# Unit 1 — From A1 Contact to A2 Exchange
**7 slots**  
Primary capabilities: C01, C02, C03, C20, C21, C24.  
Purpose: establish A2 interaction expectations immediately: linked exchange, question initiation, repair, phone/message mechanics and richer background information.

### U1-L01 — Reconnect and sustain simple small talk
Open, maintain briefly and close a familiar exchange; show interest/understanding; retrieve A1 greeting/wellbeing without re-teaching it as new.

### U1-L02 — Give a linked personal/background profile
Family, living conditions, education/job and basic personal history in a short connected series rather than isolated A1 facts.

### U1-L03 — Ask about another person's background
Question initiation + answer expansion; use a simple interview/survey frame to make C02 interactive.

### U1-L04 — Handle a basic personal phone call
Introduce self, identify intended person, answer simply, close appropriately; early prominence/intonation work.

### U1-L05 — Leave and take a simple phone message
Channel-specific message-taking plus repetition/reformulation where needed.

### U1-L06 — Repair and confirm in a familiar exchange
Ask for repetition/clarification, confirm details, check understanding and reword a short point.

### U1-L07 — Fresh contact transfer
New-person profile/contact task combining conversation + one short written/phone follow-up; no complete model copying.

# Unit 2 — People and Everyday Lives
**7 slots**  
Primary capabilities: C04, C05, C06, C25; cross-links C02/C24.  
Purpose: build richer person/life description with routines, feelings, personality, interests, work/study and skills.

### U2-L01 — Describe appearance and clothes
Understand/give a short description of appearance/clothing with A2 adjective/position support.

### U2-L02 — Describe personality and feelings
Personality/emotion language + `look/feel` description; social reactions remain pragmatic support, not the same outcome.

### U2-L03 — Describe routines and frequency more precisely
Expanded present simple, frequency, linked routine language and carry-forward from A1.

### U2-L04 — Talk about hobbies, interests and preferences with reasons
Move from `I like...` to short A2 preference/opinion + basic reason.

### U2-L05 — Talk about work/study duties and everyday activity
Work/study vocabulary, duties, current/temporary activity where natural.

### U2-L06 — Talk about skills and abilities
Ask about and describe what people can/cannot do; keep request/permission modal functions out of this performance.

### U2-L07 — Fresh person-profile transfer
Interview/observe a new person profile, then give a short linked spoken/written description using reference-building rather than repeated nouns.

# Unit 3 — Homes, Things, Possessions, and Comparison
**7 slots**  
Primary capabilities: C08, C07; cross-links C06/C09/C26.  
Purpose: extend A1 object/home language into richer description, use, reference and comparison.

### U3-L01 — Describe a home/room with richer detail
Rooms, furniture, `there is/are`, uncountables/quantity, expanded noun phrases.

### U3-L02 — Describe objects, materials, use and ownership
What something is made of/used for, possessions, position and purpose.

### U3-L03 — Compare two people/things
Core comparative system in a real choice/description task.

### U3-L04 — Use superlatives to identify/choose
Bounded superlative system for familiar sets/places/things.

### U3-L05 — Compare quantities and possessions
Quantifiers, `much/many`, `a few`, `less/more`, owned items and practical comparison.

### U3-L06 — Build clearer reference in descriptions
Relative clauses, demonstrative back-reference, pronoun substitution and noun-phrase expansion inside familiar description.

### U3-L07 — Fresh compare-and-choose transfer
Compare two unfamiliar homes/products/places and justify a simple choice using linked language.

# Unit 4 — Requests, Opinions, Advice, and Everyday Social Action
**7 slots**  
Primary capabilities: C12, C13, C06, C20; cross-cutting X05.  
Purpose: manage interpersonal action beyond A1 fixed requests: permission, refusal, reasons, advice, suggestions and pragmatic response.

### U4-L01 — Request, offer, borrow and lend
Make/respond/refuse politely in familiar situations.

### U4-L02 — Ask for/give/refuse permission and express obligation
Permission `can/be allowed`, obligation `have to/must`; keep rules/instructions cross-linked to U10.

### U4-L03 — Give an opinion and brief reason
Agreement/disagreement, stance, certainty and simple reason-giving.

### U4-L04 — Make and respond to suggestions
`let's/shall we/why don't.../could` in collaborative choice tasks.

### U4-L05 — Ask for and give basic advice
`should/could` in familiar everyday problems; avoid turning this into a modal table.

### U4-L06 — Handle apologies, excuses, compliments, encouragement and reactions
X05 pragmatic repertoire inside short real exchanges; repair/check-understanding cross-link.

### U4-L07 — Fresh decision/problem-solving transfer
Two learners/roles solve a new everyday situation using opinion + suggestion/advice + request/permission + repair.

# Unit 5 — Shopping, Food, Quantities, and Everyday Services
**7 slots**  
Primary capability: C09; cross-links C07/C12/C20/C22.  
Purpose: broaden A1 service language into more independent quantity/product/service exchange.

### U5-L01 — Ask about amounts and quantities
Countable/uncountable quantity, containers, `how much/how many`, practical number/measure retrieval.

### U5-L02 — Ask about and compare products
Colour/size/price/quality, simple product comparison and choice.

### U5-L03 — Order food and handle a simple menu exchange
Menu information + order/request + quantity/container language.

### U5-L04 — Complete a shop transaction
State what is wanted, ask price/details, pay/respond, handle a minor clarification.

### U5-L05 — Handle post-office/bank/basic service exchange
Routine direct goods/services transaction in a changed service setting.

### U5-L06 — Find information in menus, posters, price lists and service material
C22 practical information retrieval directly feeding transaction choices.

### U5-L07 — Fresh routine-transaction evidence
New service scenario with changed products/details plus a bounded misunderstanding requiring repair.

# Unit 6 — Around Town, Directions, Travel, and Reservations
**8 slots**  
Primary capabilities: C10, C11, C03, C22.  
Purpose: integrate movement/navigation with travel information and successful reservation/ticket outcomes without collapsing phone mechanics into transaction success.

### U6-L01 — Describe/find places around town
Town/place vocabulary, local map, static place relation as bridge from U3 to navigation.

### U6-L02 — Ask for directions and location
Question initiation, route destination and transport-mode choice.

### U6-L03 — Give and follow multi-step directions
Imperative/present-simple direction language, sequencing and map support.

### U6-L04 — Understand timetables, delays and transport information
Find places/times/dates/status; practical reading/listening retrieval.

### U6-L05 — Buy a ticket and ask tourist-office information
Routine travel transaction + straightforward non-specialist information.

### U6-L06 — Handle hotel/accommodation details
Understand reservation details, talk about accommodation, check in/out.

### U6-L07 — Make, change/cancel a reservation or appointment by phone
Combine C03 phone mechanics with C11 outcome; each remains separately assessable.

### U6-L08 — Fresh travel mission
New town/journey: extract information → navigate → complete a ticket/reservation step → repair one misunderstanding.

# Unit 7 — Invitations, Arrangements, Plans, and the Future
**7 slots**  
Primary capabilities: C14, C15; cross-links C12/C13/C21/C11.  
Purpose: distinguish coordinated arrangements from individual plans/intentions/predictions while reusing social action language.

### U7-L01 — Invite with time/place details; accept or decline
Spoken and written invitation routines with pragmatic response.

### U7-L02 — Make an arrangement to meet
Discuss what/where/when and reach a simple shared arrangement.

### U7-L03 — Record/read arrangements in a calendar or diary
Written planning + time/date carry-forward.

### U7-L04 — Talk about intentions with `be going to`
Canonical A2 plan/intention system, bounded to familiar future activity.

### U7-L05 — Talk/ask about scheduled and arranged future events
Present continuous/simple future uses, timetables and plan questions.

### U7-L06 — Make simple predictions and express possibility/hope
`will`, `be going to`, `may/might`, `I hope`; keep certainty boundaries simple.

### U7-L07 — Fresh weekend/event planning transfer
Use conversation + diary/message evidence to negotiate an unfamiliar plan and communicate final details.

# Unit 8 — Past Events and Personal Experiences
**6 slots**  
Primary capability: C16; cross-links C02/C24/C21.  
Purpose: extend A1 bounded past into question/negative range and present-perfect experience without forcing full narrative yet.

### U8-L01 — Expanded past simple: what happened?
Affirmative range + key time expressions and factual past event description.

### U8-L02 — Ask/answer about past times and activities
Past questions/negative responses in interview-style exchange.

### U8-L03 — Talk about life/travel experiences with `Have you ever...?`
Present perfect experience system, bounded to experiences up to now.

### U8-L04 — `yet/for/recent` bounded present-perfect patterns
Use only source-supported meanings; avoid B1-style broad present-perfect contrast claims.

### U8-L05 — Build a short experience profile
Life/travel experience plus past details; simple written/spoken follow-up.

### U8-L06 — Fresh experience interview transfer
New interview partner/profile requiring question initiation, follow-up and a short linked account.

**6 slots**

Purpose: separate narrative sequence/background from factual past exchange.

### U9-L01 — Past continuous: what was happening?
Event in progress around a past time.

### U9-L02 — Background event + main event
Past continuous background with past-simple event; source-bounded narrative control.

### U9-L03 — Sequence a short event/story
Time/sequence connectors and clear ordering.

Gist, sequence, key details, simple inference and supported unknown-word meaning.

### U9-L05 — Tell a short story/list of points

Unfamiliar picture/audio/text stimulus → reconstruct sequence → tell/write a short account.

**7 slots**  
Primary capabilities: C18, C19, C20; cross-links C13/C22.  
Purpose: practical procedural language, obligation/safety and survival health communication.

### U10-L01 — Understand and give rules/safety instructions
Affirmative/negative imperative, obligation/necessity and simple conditions.

### U10-L02 — Explain an everyday process
Ordered steps/process/recipe; linked instruction language and bounded passive support.

# Unit 11 — Messages, Media, Surveys, and Everyday Information
**7 slots**  
Primary capabilities: C21, C22, C23, C24; cross-links X03/X07/X08.  
Purpose: broaden A2 receptive/written independence and question-response formats without making a generic “reading unit”.

### U11-L01 — Read/write short personal emails, notes and messages
Everyday matters, thanks/apology, appropriate simple openings/endings.

### U11-L02 — Ask for / respond to information in writing
Simple information request/reply, including a housing-information enquiry.

### U11-L03 — Scan practical materials for facts
Posters, leaflets, brochures, menus, timetables, websites/catalogues; predictable details.

### U11-L06 — Take part in a questionnaire, survey or short interview
Understand/ask/answer questions and record simple responses.

### U11-L07 — Fresh information-to-response transfer
Gather facts from two short sources/interview data, then give a short linked spoken/written response. This is also a mediation/relay evidence point.

**7 slots**

Purpose: give C26 a bounded real performance home, then test A2 transfer without turning broad lexical topics into independent capabilities.

### U12-L01 — Describe a school/workplace/home town
Linked place description using reference, location, evaluation and familiar public-place language.

### U12-L02 — Give simple cultural/local information

X08 prepared delivery with prominence, chunking, word stress and intelligibility; familiar topic only.

Past experience/event + current context + future plan/arrangement in one bounded familiar-world task.

# Stage-05 critic

1. **83 is a baseline, not a target.** It emerged from 12 coherent performance sequences. Stage 06 may change it.
2. **Do not collapse U8 and U9 merely because both use past language.** Experience exchange and narrative-background sequencing have different task/evidence systems.
3. **Do not merge U4 and U7.** Suggestions/advice/permission and future arrangements overlap forms, but the social-action outcome differs from coordinated future planning.
4. **Do not turn U11 into a generic skills dump.** Each lesson has a distinct information/correspondence/interview performance; U11-L07 provides integration.
5. **U12 is not a “leftovers” unit.** C26 gets two bounded local/cultural performances, X08 gets a prepared-delivery home, and the remaining slots are explicit exit/integration evidence.
6. **Repair starts early.** C20 appears in U1 rather than being saved for health/travel because A2 exit explicitly requires repair.
7. **Future and past appear before the final third ends.** They need later recycle in U10–U12 rather than appearing immediately before exit.
8. **Large lexical domains do not get their own units automatically.** Environment/science/crime/technology/media content is distributed through C22/C23/C26 and relevant practical tasks.
9. **Stage 06 must test hidden overload.** Particular risk slots: U3-L06 reference building; U7-L06 future possibility/prediction; U8-L04 present-perfect bounded meanings; U11-L04 information media. Split if exact source load or independent-system count proves excessive.
10. **Transfer lessons should trend toward zero-new content.** If the exact ledger puts major new source systems into Ux-final transfer slots, reassign them earlier rather than accepting a misleading `transfer` label.
