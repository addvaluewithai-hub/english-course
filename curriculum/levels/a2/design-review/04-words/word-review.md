This file records manual curriculum decisions after reading all 871 A2 Oxford word records. It does **not** duplicate the canonical 871-row inventory, and it does **not** assign productive/receptive mastery before lesson evidence exists.

## Executive decision

- No new top-level capability is required by the A2 word inventory alone.
- No capability is removed because a topic has few new A2 words; capability authority comes first from required abilities, then phrase/grammar evidence.
- The 871 words broaden the content domains through which the 26 A2 capabilities can be learned and assessed.
- Functional/grammar-heavy lexical items such as modal verbs, pronouns, conjunctions, determiners and discourse adverbs must be controlled by the grammar/cross-cutting architecture, not taught as an isolated vocabulary dump.
- Same spelling does not imply same sense: homographs/qualifiers such as `last`, `light`, `rest`, `rock`, `ring`, etc. must remain source-record-specific in the later exact ledger.
- Final productive/receptive/grammar-owned roles are deferred to formal semantic lock after curriculum-map and lesson evidence, per the level-design playbook.

## Architecture impact after lexical review

1. **No new capability added.** New topics such as environment, crime/law, science, media and culture are content domains, not automatically new communicative endpoints.
2. **C19 health is strongly supported lexically** and remains a narrow survival-health capability rather than a general medical-English unit.
3. **C26 familiar-place/culture description is supported**, especially by town/geography/public-life/culture vocabulary, but should not become a miscellaneous “world topics” bucket.
4. **C22/C23 need broad lexical contexts** because a large share of A2 vocabulary is best learned through practical informational/descriptive texts rather than forced production.
5. **X06 and X09 remain important.** The lexical list contains substantial discourse/reference material that should improve linked output and comprehension across capabilities, not create topicless lessons.
6. **Environment/science/crime/media vocabulary must not force productive mastery merely because Oxford labels it A2.** Later semantic lock decides control from actual lesson/evidence needs.
7. **Homograph/sense control is mandatory in the exact ledger.** String-only matching is not acceptable.

## Stage-04 critic

- **Rejected:** adding “Environment”, “Crime”, “Technology”, “Science” or “Media” as top-level capabilities just because the lexical inventory is large. The required abilities do not establish those as independent communicative outcomes.
- **Rejected:** making all 871 words active vocabulary. The later semantic lock must distinguish productive, receptive and grammar-owned roles using lesson evidence.
- **Rejected:** duplicating all 871 rows into a second hand-maintained ledger. `words.json` remains the canonical record inventory; this file owns manual grouping/teaching rationale only.
- **Kept:** broad informational-reading/listening capabilities C22/C23 because they provide natural homes for lexically broad A2 content without inventing fake topic capabilities.
- **Kept:** explicit source-record identity for duplicate strings/senses; later automation may expand human mappings to IDs but must fail rather than guess.
