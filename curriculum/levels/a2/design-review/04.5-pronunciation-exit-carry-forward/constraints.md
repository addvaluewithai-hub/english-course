## 1. Pronunciation progression — 10 / 10 reviewed

A2 pronunciation remains cross-cutting. There is no standalone “pronunciation capability” or pronunciation-only unit requirement.

### P01 — Sound perception and familiar-word recognition
A2 expectation: recognise selected contrasts/familiar words across routine voices and generally clear delivery.  
Lifecycle emphasis: recycle + consolidate.  
Natural homes: C01/C03/C09/C11/C20/C22/C23.

### P02 — Consonant clarity
Maintain selected contrasts in routine phrases and short sentences; repetition remains available when needed.  
Lifecycle: recycle + practise.  
Natural homes: names/details in C02/C03, transactions C09/C11, repair C20.

### P03 — Vowel clarity and common unstressed vowels
Maintain selected vowel contrasts and begin recognising common unstressed vowels.  
Lifecycle: recycle + practise.  
Natural homes: high-frequency interaction, routine speech, travel/service language.

### P04 — Syllable shape, clusters and word endings
Produce common initial/final clusters without additions/losses that disrupt routine understanding.  
Lifecycle: recycle + consolidate.  
Natural homes: linked descriptions C02/C04/C05/C08/C26 and practical interaction.

### P05 — Audible grammatical endings
Distinguish common `-s` and `-ed` forms and recognise main pronunciation patterns.  
Lifecycle: recycle + practise.  
Natural homes: routines C05, description/comparison C04/C07, past C16/C17.

### P06 — Word stress
Use expected stress across a wider routine repertoire and notice stress changes in common related words.  
Lifecycle: recycle + practise.  
Natural homes: work/education, travel, health, technology and longer A2 lexical items.

### P07 — Sentence prominence and rhythm
**New A2 introduction/practice requirement.** Highlight key content words and reduce some predictable function words in routine short utterances.  
Natural homes: C01 social exchange, C03 phone, C09/C11 transactions, C14 arrangements, C20 repair.

### P08 — Connected speech
**New A2 introduction/practice requirement.** Understand common routine linking/reductions and use selected forms without losing key words.  
Natural homes: routine conversations, phone, transactions, arrangements and narrative/listening tasks.

### P09 — Intonation, interaction and stance
**New A2 introduction/practice requirement.** Use basic contours for statements, questions, continuation, politeness and routine turn-taking.  
Natural homes: C01/C03/C06/C12/C13/C14/C20/C24 plus X05.

### P10 — Chunking, pausing, monitoring and pronunciation repair
Group short sentences into clear units and use repetition/slower delivery/rewording in routine repair.  
Lifecycle: recycle + practise.  
Natural homes: C20 plus linked-output capabilities C02/C04/C05/C16/C17/C26.

### Pronunciation exit boundary
A2 speech should be **generally understandable in familiar situations**, while repetition and listener effort may still be needed. The course must not silently impose B1-like fluency or accent expectations.

## 2. Target A2 exit constraints

The target exit promise is:

> Complete simple routine exchanges and describe the immediate world through linked simple language.

Three mandatory fresh-evidence products from the A2 exit contract:

1. **A new routine transaction** — not a memorised replay of the taught model/scenario.
2. **A short linked description** — evidence that A2 has moved beyond isolated A1 phrases/sentences.
3. **Successful repair after a bounded misunderstanding** — learner notices/responds/clarifies enough to continue.

Additional source boundaries that must shape design:

- reception: main point/key detail of short clear everyday language;
- reading: short simple texts + predictable everyday information retrieval;
- speaking: a series of simple phrases/sentences about familiar/immediate-world content;
- writing: simple linked output with frequent connectors;
- oral interaction: routine direct information exchange and short social exchange;
- discourse: frequent connectors such as `and`, `but`, `because` plus the reviewed A2 expansion in X06/X09;
- grammar: simple structures with systematic basic mistakes still possible if meaning stays clear;
- fluency: short understandable contributions may still contain hesitation, false starts and reformulation;
- vocabulary: sufficient for routine transactions/basic communicative needs, not unrestricted topic mastery.

The exit contract is currently draft pending formal inventory/semantic lock. This stage uses it as a design constraint, not as evidence that A2 is already authorised.

## Carry-forward rules

1. A1 source records remain owned by A1; A2 references them as prerequisite/retrieval evidence only.
2. Do not spend A2 introduction budget reteaching all A1 items. Re-teach only when fresh evidence shows prerequisite weakness or when an A2 extension requires explicit contrast.
3. A2 unit/lesson mapping must schedule fresh retrieval of high-value A1 prerequisite systems before or inside their A2 extensions.
4. If A2 design exposes a genuine contradiction in locked A1, record/reopen A1 explicitly; never silently edit the previous level.
5. A2 exit is cumulative through A1+A2, but one final task does not have to independently sample every source record. The later lifecycle ledger must ensure required semantic obligations receive suitable later evidence across the course/exit portfolio.

## 4. Pre-map architecture critic after Stages 01–04.5

### Keep 26 top-level A2 capability families
Phrase, grammar, lexical, pronunciation and exit evidence do **not** currently justify adding or removing a top-level capability.

### Cross-cutting architecture is now 9 requirements
X01–X08 from Stage 01 plus grammar-forced **X09 — reference-building and noun-phrase expansion**.

### Key critic conclusions

1. **C22 vs C23 remains necessary.** Targeted information retrieval and main-point/key-detail comprehension are different evidence tasks, and the broad A2 lexical inventory gives both natural work.
2. **C16 vs C17 remains necessary.** Past event/experience exchange differs from narrative sequencing/background. Grammar (past continuous + present perfect experience) strengthens rather than collapses this distinction.
3. **C14 vs C15 remains necessary.** Coordinated arrangement with another participant differs from individual plan/intention/prediction; A2 future grammar supports both.
4. **C09 vs C11 remains necessary.** Everyday shopping/food/goods exchange differs from travel/ticket/reservation outcomes despite shared transaction language.
5. **C20 must be visible throughout the course.** Repair is also an A2 exit product; it cannot be introduced once and disappear.
6. **C26 must not become a topic dump.** Culture/environment/science/public-life vocabulary may provide content, but C26 remains familiar-place/local/cultural description, while broad factual content can live in C22/C23.
7. **No dedicated pronunciation unit.** Pronunciation is distributed against communicative load and later audited for 10/10 lifecycle coverage.
8. **A2 must visibly move from isolated A1 output to linked simple language.** X02/X06/X09 are therefore course-wide quality constraints, not optional enrichment.
9. **No lesson/unit count is authorised yet.** Counts emerge only after Stage 05 mapping and Stage 06 load/lifecycle checks.
